How to Turn Your monday.com Project Timeline into a Client-Facing Milestone Report Without Rebuilding It in PowerPoint

📅 October 21, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Your project is on track. Milestones are logged, dates are accurate, and your monday.com board tells a clean story — at least to anyone who knows how to read it.

The problem is your client doesn't.

---

The Problem

Clients don't want to log into your monday.com workspace. They don't want a screenshot of your board with seventeen columns, color-coded statuses, and team member avatars they don't recognize. What they want is a simple, professional document that answers one question: Are we on track?

So what happens? You spend 45 minutes rebuilding the same information in PowerPoint or Word. You manually copy milestone names, dates, and completion statuses. You format a table. You add a logo. You export a PDF. Then the timeline shifts by two days and you do it all over again.

This is one of the most common invisible time drains in client-facing project work — and it's entirely unnecessary.

---

The Solution

The data already lives in monday.com. Your milestone names, due dates, owners, and statuses are all sitting in structured columns, updated in real time by your team. The missing piece isn't the information — it's the presentation layer.

What you need is a way to pull that data directly from your monday.com items and render it into a formatted, client-ready document automatically. No copy-pasting. No reformatting. No version control headaches because someone updated the board after you exported the slide deck.

That's exactly what DocuAI does. It connects to your monday.com boards and generates professional documents — reports, proposals, contracts — using your item data as the source of truth. You define the template once, and every time you need a milestone report, it builds the document for you in seconds.

---

Step by Step

Here's how the workflow actually looks in practice:

1. Structure your milestone data in monday.com.

Make sure each milestone is a separate item with consistent columns: milestone name, planned date, actual/projected date, status (e.g., Complete, In Progress, At Risk), and optionally an owner or notes column.

2. Install DocuAI from the monday.com marketplace.

DocuAI connects directly to your board data — no CSV exports, no API work required.

3. Create your milestone report template.

Inside DocuAI, you build a document template that maps to your board columns. Think of it as a smart Word document where {{milestone_name}} and {{status}} are placeholders that pull live data.

4. Select the items you want to include.

You can generate a report for all milestones on a board, filter by project phase, or select specific items. This matters when you're managing multiple workstreams under one board.

5. Generate and deliver.

DocuAI produces a formatted PDF (or editable doc) you can send directly to your client. The document looks like something your team spent an hour on. It took you thirty seconds.

Real Example

A digital agency managing a website redesign project had a board with 22 milestone items tracked across four phases: Discovery, Design, Development, and Launch. Every two weeks, they owed the client a progress report.

Before DocuAI, a project manager was spending about an hour per report cycle manually building a slide deck. After setting up a milestone report template in DocuAI, the same report takes under a minute to generate. The template pulls phase, milestone name, due date, status, and a notes column — formatted into a clean, branded two-page PDF.

When the timeline shifted mid-project (as timelines do), the update was made once in monday.com. The next report reflected it automatically. No stale data. No "wait, which version did I send them?" conversations.

---

How to Get Started

If you're already tracking milestones in monday.com, you're 90% of the way there. The remaining 10% is getting the presentation layer set up — and you only have to do it once.

Start by auditing your milestone board. Make sure your columns are consistent and named clearly. If you're tracking status as free-text notes rather than a Status column, clean that up first — structured data generates better documents.

Then think about what your client actually needs to see. Usually it's: what the milestone is, when it was supposed to happen, whether it's done, and any brief context if it's delayed. That's a simple template. Simple templates generate fast.
