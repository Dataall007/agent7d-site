How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Activity Count Went Up But the Stage Never Changed — and What That Means for Your Pipeline

📅 November 5, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

There's a type of pipeline rot that's almost impossible to spot until it's too late. It doesn't look like neglect. It looks like work. Reps are logging calls, sending emails, updating notes. The activity count on those deals keeps climbing. But the stage column? Frozen.

This is one of the most common and most expensive patterns in monday.com CRMs — and most sales teams have no idea it's happening.

The Problem

Activity logging creates a false sense of momentum. When a manager reviews the board and sees deals with eight, ten, twelve activity entries, the instinct is to move on — clearly someone's working it. But activity volume and deal progress are two completely different things.

When a deal accumulates activity without advancing through stages, it usually means one of three things: a rep is avoiding a difficult conversation, the deal is functionally dead but hasn't been marked as closed-lost, or there's a genuine blocker that nobody has escalated. In all three cases, the deal is occupying space in your forecast that it hasn't earned.

The result: your pipeline looks fuller than it is, your forecast is quietly inflated, and your team is spending time on deals that have already made their decision — they just haven't told you yet.

These are your stuck deals. In monday.com CRMs, they tend to cluster invisibly inside boards that look healthy on the surface.

The Solution

You need to surface deals where activity-related columns have been updated repeatedly, but the stage column hasn't changed in a meaningful window — 30, 45, or 60 days depending on your average sales cycle.

monday.com gives you the tools to do this, but it requires knowing which columns to filter on and how to read what you find. Once you've built the right view, this stops being a blind spot and becomes a weekly hygiene check.

Step by Step

1. Identify your activity-linked columns.

On your deals board, these are typically columns like "Last Activity Date," "Activity Log," "Number of Emails," or any automation-updated columns that reflect rep touchpoints. Know which ones your team actually uses.

2. Create a filter for recent activity.

Filter for deals where the Last Activity Date is within the past 30–45 days. This confirms the rep has been touching the deal.

3. Add a stage filter.

Now layer on a filter for deals where the Stage column hasn't changed. monday.com doesn't have a native "stage unchanged for X days" filter, but you can approximate this by sorting by Stage and cross-referencing with your Last Updated column — or by using monday.com's activity log audit trail on individual items to check when Stage was last modified.

4. Set a time boundary.

You want deals with recent activity but no stage change in at least 30–45 days. Build this as a saved filter view you can return to weekly.

5. Export and triage.

Pull these deals into a focused working list. For each one: look at the actual notes, the last communication, and the stated next step. Then apply the diagnostic in the next section.

Real Example

A 12-person SaaS sales team ran this exact filter on their monday.com board. They found 17 deals, each with 8 or more activity log entries, that hadn't moved stage in 45 days. Combined pipeline value: $340,000.

When the VP of Sales reviewed them, the breakdown was roughly what you'd expect. Six deals were essentially dead — the prospect had gone cold, the rep knew it, but closing them out felt like admitting failure. Eight were stuck on procurement or legal blockers that reps hadn't escalated because they didn't want to surface a problem. Three were genuinely worth re-engaging with a different approach.

That's $340k they'd been forecasting without realizing most of it needed to be written off or escalated immediately. The filter took 20 minutes to build. The triage meeting took an hour. The pipeline became accurate by end of week.

How to Get Started

Building this filter yourself works. But it only catches one pattern. A healthy pipeline has a dozen ways to leak — unowned records, missing contact info, deals stuck at proposal with no follow-up date, stages with no activity in weeks. Doing that analysis manually across an entire board is time-consuming and easy to get wrong.
