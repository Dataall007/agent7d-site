The monday.com Pipeline Audit You Should Run Every Quarter (And Exactly How to Do It)

📅 August 21, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Most revenue doesn't disappear in one catastrophic lost deal. It bleeds out slowly — from a proposal nobody followed up on, a contact record with no owner, a deal that's been sitting in "Negotiation" for eleven weeks because everyone assumed someone else was handling it.

A quarterly monday.com pipeline audit is how you stop that bleeding before it compounds into a quarter you can't explain.

Here's exactly how to run one.

---

The Problem

Pipelines go stale in four predictable ways.

1. Stage rot. Deals stop moving but nobody archives them. Your pipeline looks full; your forecast is fiction.

2. Ownership gaps. A rep leaves, a lead comes in over the weekend, a deal gets reassigned mid-cycle — and somewhere in that shuffle, a record ends up with no owner. Nobody is working it because nobody knows it's theirs.

3. Missing data. Close date, deal value, company size — fields that exist in your board but are blank for 40% of your records. Blank fields don't just look messy. They make forecasting impossible and automation unreliable.

4. Zombie next-action dates. A "next action" date from six weeks ago that nobody updated. The deal looks active. It isn't.

Each of these is a minor problem when it's one record. It becomes a revenue problem when it's thirty records and you don't know which thirty.

---

The Solution

A structured, repeatable quarterly audit that takes about two hours and produces a prioritized list of deals to fix, follow up, or close out.

The goal isn't perfection. It's visibility. You want to know, once per quarter, exactly what shape your pipeline is in — not what shape you hope it's in.

---

Step by Step

1. Audit the five fields that are almost always incomplete.

Sort every deal board by these columns and count the blanks:

  • Close Date — Without this, you have no forecast. Deals without close dates are wishes, not pipeline.
  • Deal Value — Can't prioritize, can't project revenue.
  • Owner — Unowned deals get worked by nobody.
  • Next Action Date — The single best signal of whether a deal is actually alive.
  • Lead Source — Incomplete for half of most pipelines, critical for understanding which channels actually convert.

Fill what you can. Flag what you can't, and assign someone to chase down the missing information.

2. Find stuck deals using filters and date columns.

In your CRM board on monday.com, filter by stage. Then sort by "Last Updated" or a custom "Stage Entry Date" column if you've built one. Any deal that hasn't moved stages in more than 21 days deserves a look. Beyond 45 days, it's a red flag.

3. Find unowned records.

Filter your board: Owner is empty. Whatever appears in that list is flying without a pilot. Assign every record to a rep or mark it disqualified. No middle ground.

4. Build a pipeline health dashboard.

Create a monday.com dashboard with four widgets: deals by stage (to spot stage rot), deals by owner (to spot imbalance), deals missing close date (a simple filter widget), and average deal age per stage. Twenty minutes to build. Permanent visibility.

---

Real Example

A client came to us mid-Q3 with a pipeline that looked healthy — 34 open deals, solid average deal size, reasonable close-rate history.

We ran an audit. One deal had been in "Proposal Sent" for 47 days. No next-action date. No assigned owner. The original rep had left the company six weeks earlier, and the deal had been reassigned in monday.com — but the reassignment notification went to an inbox nobody monitored.

The prospect hadn't gone cold. They'd sent two follow-up emails that sat unanswered. When the new rep called them the day we surfaced the record, they closed within a week.

That's not a lucky outcome. That's what happens when you actually look.

---

How to Get Started

Run your audit this week. Use the checklist below as your repeatable template:

  • [ ] Filter all deals with no Close Date
  • [ ] Filter all deals with no Deal Value
  • [ ] Filter all deals with no Owner
  • [ ] Filter all deals with no Next Action Date — or one older than 14 days
  • [ ] Sort each stage by Last Updated — flag anything over 21 days
  • [ ] Review and resolve all records flagged above
  • [ ] Update or rebuild your pipeline health dashboard

Block two hours. Do it quarterly. Put it in the calendar now.
