How to Auto-Generate a Vendor or Subcontractor Agreement from Your monday.com Project Data

📅 September 7, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

If your team hires subcontractors or freelancers regularly, you already know the pattern: a new engagement gets confirmed, someone opens last month's agreement, does a "save as," and spends the next half hour hunting through monday.com to pull the right scope, rate, and deadline into a Word doc. Then they email it. Then they wait. It's tedious, error-prone, and completely unnecessary in 2024.

Here's how to generate that agreement automatically — directly from your monday.com board data.

---

The Problem

Subcontractor agreements are the most re-typed document in project-based businesses, and the reason is structural. The agreement itself barely changes. Your payment terms, liability clauses, IP ownership language, and governing law are essentially static. What changes every single time is a small set of project-specific variables: who you're hiring, for what, at what rate, and by when.

Because that variable data lives in monday.com and the document lives in Word, someone has to manually bridge the gap every time. For a marketing agency running six to ten campaigns at once — each with its own freelance designer — that's easily two to four hours a week of ops work that creates zero value. Worse, manual re-entry means version drift: wrong rates, stale scope descriptions, or last month's deadline sitting in a "new" contract.

---

The Solution

Map your monday.com board columns directly to the variable fields in your agreement template. Once the mapping exists, document generation becomes a triggered event — not a manual task. When a subcontractor item is created or a status moves to "Confirmed," the agreement generates itself, pre-populated with the correct data.

The tool that handles this inside monday.com is DocuAI. It sits between your board and your document template, reads the column values you specify, and produces a finished, correctly populated agreement — ready for e-signature or PDF delivery.

---

Step by Step

1. Identify the five fields that change every time — and confirm they're already on your board.

For most subcontractor agreements, these are:

  • Subcontractor name (Text or People column)
  • Project or campaign name (Text column)
  • Scope of work (Long Text column)
  • Agreed rate and payment schedule (Numbers + Text)
  • Start and end dates (Date column)

If any of these are missing from your board, add them before you build the template. Your agreement will only be as complete as your board data.

2. Build your reusable agreement template with merge fields.

Take your standard agreement — the one your ops manager has been copying manually — and replace every variable with a merge field tag. In DocuAI, these look like {{subcontractor_name}}, {{project_scope}}, {{end_date}}, and so on. The rest of the document — your legal boilerplate — stays exactly as written.

3. Map each merge field to a monday.com column in DocuAI.

Inside the DocuAI configuration panel, you connect each template tag to the corresponding column on your board. This mapping is saved once and reused every time.

4. Set your automation trigger.

Use monday.com's native automation builder to trigger DocuAI when a specific condition is met — for example: When a status changes to "Confirmed" in the Subcontractors group, generate document using [Template Name]. You can also trigger on item creation if your workflow confirms at the point of adding.

5. Choose your output and delivery method.

DocuAI packages the populated agreement as a PDF or a document ready for your e-signature workflow. It can attach it directly to the monday.com item, send it to a specified email, or both.

Real Example

A mid-size marketing agency hires freelance designers for every campaign — typically two to four per month. Their ops manager was spending 20 to 30 minutes per engagement manually copying project scope, payment terms, and deadlines from the campaign board into their standard designer agreement.

After setting up DocuAI with a mapped template and a "Confirmed" status trigger, that same agreement generates in under ten seconds. The ops manager's only remaining task is reviewing the output before it goes out. Total time: two minutes. The agency now runs the same process across all project types — video production, copywriting, dev retainers — each with its own template variant.

On version control: DocuAI lets you maintain multiple template versions and assign each to a specific board, group, or tag. A "Design Freelancer Agreement v3" fires for design projects; a "Development Retainer Agreement v2" fires for tech engagements. No manual template selection required.

---

How to Get Started

Before you touch any tooling, do this first: download your current subcontractor agreement and highlight every field that changes between engagements. Then open your monday.com board and confirm each of those fields has a dedicated column. If the data is clean and consistent on the board, the automation will work cleanly every time.

Free checklist — variable fields to capture for any subcontractor agreement:

  • [ ] Full legal name of subcontractor
  • [ ] Business entity type (if applicable)
  • [ ] Project / campaign name
  • [ ] Scope of work (detailed description)
  • [ ] Deliverables and acceptance criteria
  • [ ] Project start date
  • [ ] Project end date / deadline
  • [ ] Rate (hourly or fixed)
  • [ ] Payment schedule (milestone, net-30, etc.)
  • [ ] Invoice submission instructions
  • [ ] Confidentiality scope
  • [ ] IP ownership clause trigger (yes/no)
  • [ ] Governing law / jurisdiction

Get those columns on your board, build the template once, and you're done with manual agreement prep.
