The Duplicate Deal Problem in monday.com — How to Find Them, Merge What Matters, and Stop Them Coming Back

📅 September 4, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

The Problem

Duplicate deal records in monday.com are a silent pipeline killer. They don't announce themselves. They sit there looking like legitimate opportunities while quietly doing damage underneath the surface.

Here's what actually happens when duplicates go unchecked. Your pipeline value is inflated — you think you have $800K in active deals, but $90K of that is the same five companies entered twice. Your activity history gets split across two items, so neither record tells the full story of a relationship. And worst of all, two reps can be actively working the same prospect without either of them knowing, burning goodwill with a contact who's suddenly hearing from your company twice a week.

In a monday.com CRM, this is more common than most teams realize. monday.com doesn't have a native duplicate detection engine. When a rep creates a new deal, nothing stops them from creating one that already exists under a slightly different company name, a different email format, or a contact name spelled differently. Scale that across a team of six or eight people working fast, and duplicates accumulate quickly.

The problem compounds during imports. Any time you pull data from a spreadsheet, a previous tool, or a marketing platform, duplicates come along for the ride and land directly in your pipeline board.

The Solution

The fix has two parts: a one-time cleanup of existing duplicates, and a set of simple practices that prevent new ones from forming.

For the cleanup, you need to systematically surface records that likely represent the same deal — then decide which one to keep, what to merge, and what to delete. For prevention, you need a short intake workflow and a naming convention your team actually follows.

Neither of these requires a developer or an expensive third-party integration. You can do most of this inside monday.com today.

Step by Step

1. Export your pipeline board to a spreadsheet.

Go to your CRM board, click the three-dot menu, and export to Excel or CSV. You want every item with its company name, contact name, and deal owner visible.

2. Sort and scan for near-matches.

Sort by company name. Duplicates often hide as "Acme Corp" and "Acme Corporation," or "John Smith - Inbound" and "John Smith - LinkedIn." Your eye will catch most of them faster than any algorithm at this scale.

3. Flag suspicious pairs.

In your spreadsheet, tag any row where the company name or contact is a likely match. Don't delete anything yet — you need to check both records in monday.com before deciding which to keep.

4. Compare records side by side.

Open both items in monday.com. Check the activity log, notes, file attachments, and connected contacts. The one with the richer history is your primary record.

5. Consolidate manually.

Copy any unique notes or files from the secondary record into the primary. Update the primary with the correct owner and stage. Then archive or delete the secondary.

6. Set a naming convention and make it visible.

Create a pinned update or a board description that defines how deals should be named — for example: "Company Name — Contact Last Name — Source." Simple, visible, enforced.

7. Add a quick check to your intake process.

Before any rep creates a new deal item, they search the board for the company name. Make this a one-line step in your team's onboarding checklist.

Real Example

A recruitment firm using monday.com as their CRM ran a quarterly pipeline review and discovered that 11% of their active pipeline items were duplicates — the same candidate placements or employer deals entered by different consultants at different stages of outreach. Two senior consultants had independently been building relationships with the same hiring manager at a logistics company. Neither knew. The deal closed, but nearly went sideways when the hiring manager mentioned in a final call that he'd "spoken to three people from your firm this month." Eleven percent pipeline inflation. One near-miss deal. Dozens of wasted hours. That's the real cost.

After a two-hour cleanup session using the method above and a simple naming convention, duplicate creation dropped to near zero within a month.
