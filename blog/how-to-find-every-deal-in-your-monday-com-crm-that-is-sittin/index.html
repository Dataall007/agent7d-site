How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM That Is Sitting in Discovery or Qualification with a Close Date in the Past

📅 October 13, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Most pipeline problems are visible if you know where to look. This one hides in plain sight — and it's one of the most common reasons sales forecasts are wrong before the month even starts.

---

The Problem

A deal sitting in Discovery or Qualification with a close date that passed three months ago is not an active opportunity. It is a record. It is occupying a line in your pipeline, contributing to your coverage number, and giving your forecast zero useful signal.

The danger isn't just inaccuracy. It's the false confidence that comes with seeing a healthy-looking pipeline total. If your board says $1.2M in open pipeline and $300K of that is stale early-stage deals with expired close dates, you are not managing a $1.2M pipeline. You are managing a $900K pipeline with a data problem.

Early-stage deals are the most dangerous category for this kind of rot because they require the least commitment to create. A rep logs a discovery call, creates the deal, sets an optimistic close date, and moves on. If the prospect goes quiet, the deal doesn't — it just sits. Monday.com does not automatically close records when close dates pass. It keeps them exactly where you left them, adding them to every report you run.

---

The Solution

You need a saved view in your monday.com CRM board that surfaces every deal where the stage is Discovery or Qualification and the close date is before today. Not a one-time export. A persistent, named view that your team sees every week.

Once the view exists, you run a three-option decision on each record: requalify it, move it to a nurture status, or close it as lost. The goal is a pipeline where every open deal has a close date that reflects an actual working timeline, not a forgotten estimate from six months ago.

---

Step by Step

1. Open your deals board in monday.com.

Navigate to the board where your pipeline lives.

2. Create a new view.

Click the plus icon next to your existing views and select "Table View." Name it something unambiguous: Stale Early-Stage Deals works.

3. Apply your first filter.

Use the Filter menu to filter by your stage column. Select Discovery and Qualification (or whatever your equivalent early-stage labels are). Set the condition to "is any of."

4. Add the close date filter.

Add a second filter condition for your close date column. Set the condition to "is before" and use a dynamic date value of "today." This makes the filter self-updating — you do not need to manually change dates each week.

5. Review the last activity column.

If you track last activity date on your board (you should), sort by it. Deals with no activity in 60-plus days are almost certainly abandoned. Deals with recent activity may simply have slipped their timeline and are still workable.

6. Apply your decision framework.

For each deal: if there has been meaningful contact in the last 30 days and the prospect is still engaged, update the close date and requalify. If the prospect has gone quiet but the need is real, move the deal to a nurture status so it stays visible without polluting your forecast. If there is no realistic path forward, close it as lost. Closed-lost records with honest notes are more valuable than open records with no information.

7. Set a recurring reminder.

Pin this view and add a monday.com automation or a calendar reminder to review it weekly. The deals that show up here next month will tell you something about your qualification process, not just your data hygiene.

---

Real Example

A sales manager at a managed IT services company ran a pipeline export during a quarterly review. The total open pipeline looked reasonable — enough to justify the board presentation. When she filtered for Discovery and Qualification deals with close dates in the past, she found nineteen records. The oldest had a close date fourteen months prior. The most recent was three months ago. All nineteen were still marked open. All nineteen were included in the monthly pipeline total.

After applying the decision framework, two were requalified with updated timelines after a quick call revealed the prospect had simply been through a budget freeze. Four moved to nurture. Thirteen were closed lost. The pipeline total dropped. The forecast accuracy — measured against actual close rates two quarters later — improved significantly because the number reflected real opportunities, not historical noise.

---

How to Get Started

Build the view today. It takes under ten minutes. The harder part is the decision conversation with your team about what the number is actually supposed to represent — a wish list or a working forecast.

If you want to understand the full scope of the problem before you start cleaning, it helps to see it quantified. How many records have no owner? How many deals have been in the same stage for more than 90 days? How much of your pipeline total disappears when you remove records with missing or expired data?
