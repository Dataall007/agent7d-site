How to Generate a Vendor or Subcontractor Brief Directly from Your monday.com Project Board

📅 September 11, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

monday.com subcontractor brief automation | agent7d.com

---

The Problem

Every time a new vendor or subcontractor joins a project, someone on your team has to write a brief. Scope of work. Deliverables. Deadlines. Key contacts. Brand guidelines. Payment terms. The document is different every time, but the process is identical every time — open a blank doc, pull up the monday.com board, copy the information across manually, format it, and send it.

If you're running three projects simultaneously and each one brings in two or three freelancers or subcontractors, that's potentially nine briefing documents a month, all written from scratch, all pulling from data that already exists on your board.

The real cost isn't just the time it takes to write one brief. It's the inconsistency that creeps in when different team members write them differently, the errors that happen when someone copies a deadline wrong, and the delay that occurs when the person who usually does it is unavailable. A freelancer sitting idle waiting for their brief is a timeline risk — and it's an entirely avoidable one.

---

The Solution

Your monday.com project board already contains everything a vendor brief needs. The deliverables are in a column. The deadline is in a column. The point of contact is in a column. The brand asset links are in a column. The scope notes are in a column.

The missing piece isn't the information — it's a way to take that structured data and push it into a formatted document the moment it's needed, without anyone touching a keyboard.

That's what DocuAI for monday.com does. It connects directly to your board, maps your column data to a document template you define once, and generates a complete, professionally formatted vendor brief automatically — triggered by a status change, a button click, or a monday.com automation rule.

---

Step by Step

1. Audit your board columns against what a brief actually needs.

A standard vendor brief covers: project name, scope of work, specific deliverables, deadlines, file format requirements, brand guidelines, point of contact, and revision policy. Walk through your existing board and confirm each of these has a dedicated column. If deliverables are buried in a long text field, split them out. Clean structure now means clean output later.

2. Create a document template in DocuAI.

Build your brief template once — your agency letterhead, your standard sections, your preferred layout. Anywhere you'd normally type in project-specific information, insert a dynamic field that maps to the corresponding monday.com column. {{Deliverables}}, {{Final Deadline}}, {{Brand Guidelines Link}}, {{Account Manager}} — each placeholder pulls live data from the item.

3. Set the trigger.

In your monday.com automations, set a rule: when the Vendor Status column changes to Confirmed, trigger DocuAI to generate the brief for that item. The document is created, stored, and a link is added back to the item — all without manual input.

4. Share the brief.

DocuAI can automatically email the generated PDF to the contact listed in the vendor's row, or simply make it available for your team to send. Either way, the brief is ready within seconds of confirmation.

Real Example

A mid-size marketing agency brings in freelance designers on a per-project basis. Their creative director was spending 20–30 minutes writing a scope brief for each new designer — pulling deliverables from the project board, confirming deadlines, linking brand assets, and writing it all into a Google Doc before emailing it.

After setting up DocuAI, their monday.com project board now does this automatically. The board has columns for Deliverables, Final Deadline, Brand Guidelines (a file link column), Point of Contact, and Revision Rounds. The moment the creative director marks a designer's status as Confirmed, DocuAI generates a clean, branded briefing document with all of that data populated, and sends it directly to the designer's email address stored in the board.

First brief they tested took four seconds to generate. Same information, zero manual effort.
