How to Generate a Subcontractor Completion Certificate Directly from Your monday.com Project Board

📅 September 28, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Most project managers know the feeling. The subcontractor finishes on Friday, the site manager signs off in the app, and then nothing happens until someone remembers to draft the completion certificate on Tuesday. By then the subcontractor is chasing payment, the client wants confirmation, and you're hunting through monday.com trying to remember which column held the acceptance date.

The certificate itself takes maybe 20 minutes to write. The delay costs you far more.

The Problem

A subcontractor completion certificate is not a complicated document. It confirms who did what, when it was completed, who accepted the work, and on what terms. But because it lives outside monday.com — usually in a shared Word template somewhere on a network drive — it depends entirely on someone remembering to act.

That gap between "work done" and "paperwork done" is where projects stall. Subcontractors cannot invoice final retention until the certificate exists. Clients withhold sign-off on the broader project until each trade is formally closed out. And on a fit-out or construction job with six trades running in parallel, that delay multiplies fast.

The manual process also introduces errors. Dates get transposed. The wrong subcontractor name ends up in the wrong document. Deliverable descriptions don't match the scope reference in the contract. These are small mistakes with real consequences — disputed invoices, rework claims, and strained relationships.

The Solution

Your monday.com board already holds most of the data a completion certificate needs. The issue is that nothing connects that data to the document. DocuAI bridges that gap by mapping your board columns directly to a certificate template and generating a formatted, ready-to-sign document the moment a status column hits "Complete."

No copy-pasting. No switching applications. The certificate is produced from live board data, attached to the item, and ready to send — all within monday.com.

Step by Step

1. Audit your current board columns.

A completion certificate needs: subcontractor name, scope reference or trade description, agreed deliverables, scheduled completion date, actual completion date, sign-off person, and acceptance notes. Most boards have some of these. Few have all of them with consistent naming.

2. Add the missing columns.

Create a text column for Scope Reference (linked to your contract or SOW number), a date column for Actual Completion Date, a people column for Authorised Signatory, and a long-text column for Acceptance Notes. If you're using monday's subitems for deliverables, make sure each one has a clear status.

3. Set up a naming convention.

Consistency matters for automation. Use the same column names across all project boards — "Subcontractor," "Scope Ref," "Actual Completion," "Signatory," "Acceptance Notes." This lets you reuse the same DocuAI template across every project without remapping.

4. Build your certificate template in DocuAI.

Create a document template using placeholders that match your column names — {Subcontractor}, {ScopeRef}, {ActualCompletion}, and so on. DocuAI pulls the live values from the item and drops them into the correct positions. Include your company letterhead, a signature block, and a standard certificate footer.

5. Set the trigger.

In DocuAI, configure generation to fire automatically when the item's Status column changes to "Complete." The certificate is created, saved as a PDF, and linked back to the monday.com item — no manual step required.

6. Route for signature and store.

Send the generated PDF directly to the authorised signatory for e-signature, then attach the signed version back to the item. Every certificate lives exactly where the project data lives.

Real Example

A fit-out contractor managing six trades — flooring, electrical, joinery, glazing, HVAC, and AV — needed to issue completion certificates to each subcontractor on the day their work was signed off. Previously, the project coordinator drafted certificates manually, often two or three days after sign-off, which delayed retention invoices and frustrated subcontractors.

After restructuring their monday.com board with the columns above and connecting DocuAI, the process changed completely. When the site manager marks the HVAC subcontractor "Complete" and enters the acceptance notes, the certificate generates automatically, attaches to the item, and goes out for signature — same day, every time. Six trades, six certificates, zero manual drafting.

How to Get Started

Start with one project board. Audit your columns against the list above, add what's missing, and build a single certificate template in DocuAI. Run it on your next subcontractor sign-off. Once you see a complete, formatted certificate appear the moment you change a status, you won't go back to the Word template.
