How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Same Contact Is Linked to a Competitor — and Why It's Quietly Poisoning Your Pipeline

📅 October 31, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

There is a specific kind of pipeline rot that is almost impossible to see without deliberately looking for it. It does not show up on your activity reports. Your rep does not flag it. Your forecast does not know it exists. But it is costing you real money in wasted time, skewed win rates, and proposals that were never going to close.

The problem: a contact in your monday.com CRM who is associated with a company you have already identified as a competitor.

The Problem

This is more common than most sales leaders realize, and it happens through predictable channels.

People change jobs. A champion at a mid-market company you were chasing moves to a direct competitor. Your rep does not notice because they are focused on the deal, not the contact's LinkedIn profile. The deal stays open. The contact is still responsive — because now they are gathering competitive intelligence on your behalf, for free.

Companies get acquired. A prospect you have been nurturing for six months gets bought by a company you have flagged as a competitor. The deal does not disappear from your board. It just quietly dies, while your rep continues touching it.

Shared domains and referrals create false signals. A contact at a partner company introduces someone from their network. That contact happens to work at a competitor. The deal gets created because the introduction felt warm, and no one cross-references the company board.

In each scenario, the deal corrupts your pipeline in two ways: it inflates your forecast with revenue that has virtually no chance of closing, and it absorbs rep time and energy that should go toward winnable opportunities.

The Solution

The fix is a deliberate cross-reference audit between three boards in your monday.com workspace: your Contacts board, your Companies board, and your Deals board.

The goal is simple — surface every deal where the primary contact's linked company appears anywhere in your competitor list. Once you have those records, you can make informed decisions about whether to close the deal, reassign it, or flag it for review.

This is not a technical project. It requires no integrations, no automations (at first), and no external tools. What it does require is that your competitor companies are actually documented in monday.com, which is a hygiene issue worth fixing immediately if they are not.

Step by Step

1. Create or verify your competitor list. In your Companies board, add a column called "Company Type" or "Relationship" if you do not already have one. Tag every known competitor clearly — use a Status column with a dedicated label like "Competitor."

2. Pull all contacts linked to competitor companies. Filter your Contacts board to show only records where the linked company has that competitor status. Export or note every contact name and their associated deals.

3. Cross-reference against your Deals board. In your Deals board, filter by the contacts you identified in step two. You are looking for deals that are still open — any stage, any value.

4. Review each match against four criteria: current deal stage, date of last activity, assigned rep, and whether the contact's company affiliation was ever noted or flagged anywhere in the record. Pay particular attention to deals in Proposal or Negotiation — those are where the wasted effort is most acute.

5. Build a saved filter or dashboard view that automatically surfaces deals where the primary contact's company is tagged as a competitor. Check it monthly. Set a monday.com automation to notify a manager when a new deal is created with a contact linked to a competitor company.

Real Example

A SaaS sales team running a monday.com CRM conducted this audit during a quarterly pipeline review. They found three open deals — combined value just over $180,000 — where the primary contact had changed employers in the previous 90 days. All three contacts had moved to companies listed in the team's own competitor board.

None of the reps knew. Two of the deals were in proposal stage. One rep had sent a custom pricing deck four days earlier.

Once identified, the team closed two deals immediately and reassigned the third (the contact had moved into a non-competing division of a larger conglomerate that happened to share a parent company with a competitor). They recovered the time those reps were spending and redistributed it to three deals that closed within 45 days.

The revenue was not lost in those three deals. It was never there. The audit just made that visible before more resources were spent.

How to Get Started

Begin with your Companies board today. If your competitors are not documented and tagged, do that first — it is the foundation everything else depends on. Then run the cross-reference manually using the steps above. Even a rough first pass will surface records you did not know were compromised.

This kind of audit is also one of several diagnostic checks that reveals whether your monday.com CRM data is actually trustworthy enough to forecast from. Competitor-linked contacts are one leak. Unowned records, stale deals with no activity, and missing contact information are others — and they compound each other in ways that are invisible until you look.
