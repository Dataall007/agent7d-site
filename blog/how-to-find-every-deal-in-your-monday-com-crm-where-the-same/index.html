How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Same Email Domain Appears Across Multiple Companies

📅 October 25, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Duplicate company records in monday.com CRM are not a minor housekeeping issue. They are a revenue problem — and one that's almost always invisible until it's embarrassing or expensive. This post shows you exactly how to find them using email domain patterns, before they do real damage.

---

The Problem

Duplicate company records don't appear because your team is careless. They appear because monday.com CRMs grow organically. One rep imports a CSV. Another adds a contact manually. A third creates a company from an inbound lead form. Nobody checks whether the account already exists. Over time, "Acme Corp" and "Acme Corporation Ltd" are both sitting in your board — each with their own contacts, their own deals, and their own assigned rep.

The insidious part is that both records look legitimate. Both have activity. Both have notes. There's no obvious error flag in the interface. The duplication is hiding in plain sight inside the company name field, where minor variations — abbreviations, legal suffixes, punctuation — are enough to fool any alphabetical sort.

Email domains cut through all of that. Everyone at Acme Corp and Acme Corporation Ltd uses @acmecorp.com. If that domain appears under two different company names in your monday.com CRM, you almost certainly have a duplicate.

---

The Solution

Stop relying on company name matching to find duplicates. Names are inconsistent by nature. Email domains are not. A company's domain is effectively a unique identifier — stable, standardized, and already sitting inside your contact records.

The audit approach is simple: extract the email domain from every contact record in your monday.com CRM, group those domains by company, and flag any domain that maps to more than one company name. That list is your duplicate candidates. It won't be perfect — legitimate subsidiaries and holding companies can share domains — but it will surface every case worth investigating, fast.

---

Step by Step

1. Export your contacts board. From your monday.com contacts board, export to Excel or CSV. You need at minimum: contact email, linked company name, deal value if connected.

2. Extract the email domain. In Excel, use a formula to isolate everything after the @ symbol. In Excel: =MID(A2,FIND("@",A2)+1,LEN(A2)). This gives you the domain for every contact row.

3. Build a pivot table grouped by domain. Rows: email domain. Values: distinct count of company names linked to that domain. Any domain with a count greater than one is a duplicate candidate.

4. Cross-reference open deals. Go back to your monday.com deals board. Filter by the company names flagged in step three. Note deal stages, assigned owners, and deal values. This is where the financial exposure becomes visible.

5. Investigate and decide. For each flagged domain, decide: true duplicate, legitimate subsidiary, or acquisition? True duplicates need to be merged. Subsidiaries need a clear parent-child structure. Acquisitions need a historical note and a single going-forward record.

6. Merge in monday.com. monday.com doesn't have a native merge function for company records, so merging is manual: move all contacts and deals to the primary record, update the company name to your chosen standard, then archive or delete the secondary record.

7. Add a domain field to your company board. Going forward, make email domain a standard field on your Companies board in monday.com. Use column permissions or automations to prompt reps to check for existing records before creating a new company.

---

Real Example

A B2B software company doing a pipeline review found two separate company records in their monday.com CRM: "Acme Corp" and "Acme Corporation Ltd." Both were active. Both had open deals in the negotiation stage. Two different reps were working them independently — neither aware the other existed.

When they ran the domain-level audit, both companies resolved to @acmecorp.com. Pulling the deal records together revealed the worst-case outcome: one rep had already sent a proposal. The other rep was preparing a second proposal — at a price point $18,000 lower, based on different assumptions about contract length. The prospect had two proposals from the same vendor, at different prices, from two different people who didn't know about each other. The deal went cold.

The fix took an afternoon. The prevention — adding a mandatory domain field and a duplicate-check automation in monday.com — took another hour.

---

How to Get Started

The domain audit above works if you have a few hundred records and time to do it manually. If your monday.com CRM has thousands of contacts, multiple boards, and a pipeline that's accumulated years of organic growth, the manual version will take days — and you'll probably miss things.
