How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM That Is Assigned to Someone Who Has Left the Company

📅 September 12, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

The Problem

Someone on your sales team leaves. You offboard them, remove their monday.com seat, maybe reassign their open tasks. And then… nothing. The deals they owned just sit there.

This happens at every company, and it is more damaging than most sales leaders realize. When a rep leaves, their pipeline doesn't disappear — it quietly dies. Deals stay in whatever stage they were in on the last day that person logged in. No one follows up. No one sends the proposal. No one takes the next step. The prospect eventually goes cold or signs with a competitor, and your CRM never tells you why.

The real problem is visibility. monday.com doesn't alert you when a board item is owned by a deactivated user. There's no native warning that says "this deal has no active owner." The deal just sits there, looking like a normal pipeline record, while the revenue attached to it silently walks out the door.

If your company has had any turnover in the last 12 months — even one person — you almost certainly have orphaned deals in your pipeline right now.

The Solution

The fix is straightforward, but you have to be deliberate about it. The goal is to surface every deal in your monday.com CRM that is either assigned to a deactivated user or has no owner assigned at all.

Once you can see those deals in one place, you can triage them: some will be worth rescuing with immediate outreach, some will be dead, and some will need to be moved to a different stage or closed out entirely. But you cannot do any of that until you find them.

There are two ways to approach this — manually, using monday.com's built-in filters, or automatically, using a tool that scans your pipeline and flags the gaps for you.

Step by Step

Here is how to do this manually inside monday.com:

1. Check your team member list first. Go to your account settings and look at who has been deactivated. Write down their names — you'll need them for filtering.

2. Open your CRM board. Navigate to the board where your deals live.

3. Filter by Person column. Use the Filter function and select your "Owner" or "Assigned to" column. Filter to show only the deactivated users you identified. Do this one name at a time if needed.

4. Create a saved filter for "No Owner." Also filter for records where the person column is empty. These are your completely unowned deals — often the most neglected.

5. Export or tag the results. Once you've found these deals, either export them to a spreadsheet or create a dedicated Group on your board called "Needs Reassignment." Move everything there so it's visible and actionable.

6. Assign a new owner immediately. Don't let these sit. Pick up the phone or send an email within 24 hours — the longer a deal goes untouched, the colder it gets.

Repeat this process for every deactivated user. If you've had significant turnover, budget a few hours for this.

Real Example

A SaaS company with an eight-person sales team ran this exercise after their top rep left mid-quarter. When they filtered their monday.com CRM for deals owned by that rep, they found 23 open opportunities totaling roughly $180,000 in pipeline value. Fourteen of those deals were in late stages — demos completed, proposals sent, just waiting on follow-up that never came.

They reached out to all 14 within 48 hours. Six responded. Two closed within three weeks. That's real revenue that would have been written off as lost without a single word of explanation.

The other nine deals in earlier stages were reassigned and continued through the pipeline normally. None of this required a new tool or a big process overhaul. It required knowing where to look.

How to Get Started

If you want to do this manually, the steps above will get you there. If your team is larger or you've had more turnover, the manual process gets tedious fast — and it's easy to miss boards, miss columns, or miss deactivated users who were removed from the system months ago.

The faster path is to let a tool do the scanning for you.
