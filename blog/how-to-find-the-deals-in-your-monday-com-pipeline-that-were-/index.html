How to Find the Deals in Your monday.com Pipeline That Were Moved Backwards Through Stages — and What That Pattern Reveals

📅 October 9, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Most sales managers spend their time watching deals move forward. Conversion rates, velocity, win rates — all forward-looking metrics. But the deals quietly sliding backwards through your monday.com pipeline? Those are the ones quietly wrecking your forecast accuracy and inflating your average deal length.

Stage regression — when a deal moves from a later stage back to an earlier one — is one of the most underexamined signals in B2B sales operations. And in monday.com, it's hiding in plain sight.

The Problem

Backward stage movement is more common than most sales managers realise, for one simple reason: monday.com makes it easy to move deals in any direction, at any time, with no friction and no required explanation. That flexibility is great for speed. It's terrible for data integrity.

The result is pipelines where deals silently regress — from "Proposal Sent" back to "Qualifying," from "Commercial Review" back to "Scoping" — and nobody flags it because there's no native alert. The deal is still in the pipeline. It's still assigned. It just lost two stages and three weeks, and your forecast absorbed that quietly.

The danger isn't just wasted time. Deals that move backward are telling you something is structurally wrong — with your qualification process, your stage definitions, your team's advancement discipline, or the customer's internal situation. Ignoring the pattern means the same problem repeats, deal after deal.

The Solution

monday.com's activity log and column tracking features give you everything you need to detect stage regression — you just have to know where to look and how to structure it.

The approach combines two things: a retrospective audit of historical stage movement using monday.com's activity log, and a forward-looking dashboard view that automatically surfaces deals whose current stage is earlier than their previous highest stage. Once you have both, you stop managing by gut feel and start managing by pattern.

Step by Step

1. Enable column tracking on your Stage column.

Go to your CRM board settings, select the Status column you use for deal stages, and enable "Track changes over time." If this isn't already on, turn it on now — it won't backfill, but it starts recording from that point forward.

2. Audit backward movement using the Activity Log.

Open any deal item and click the Activity Log. Filter by column changes to the Stage column. You'll see every stage transition with a timestamp and the user who made the change. For a pipeline-wide view, export your board activity via monday.com's API or use a third-party integration (Coupler.io works well here) to pull all Stage column history into a spreadsheet.

3. Build a formula column to flag regression.

Add a Formula column to your board. Using monday.com's formula engine, compare the current stage (mapped to a numeric value) against the highest stage the deal has previously reached. Deals where current stage value is lower than historical peak get flagged. You'll need to maintain a numeric mapping of your stages as a reference.

4. Create a dashboard view called "Stage Regression Watch."

Build a filtered board view that shows only flagged deals, grouped by assigned rep and sorted by how many stages they've regressed. Review this view weekly in your pipeline call. Make regression visible — naming it as a metric changes how seriously reps treat stage advancement.

5. Log the reason for every backward move.

Add a required text column: "Regression Reason." When a deal moves back, the rep must document why. After 30 days, those reasons become your diagnostic data.

Real Example

A mid-market IT reseller using monday.com noticed their average deal length was running longer than benchmarks but couldn't identify why. After pulling 90 days of stage history from their board's activity log, a clear pattern emerged: deals were regularly regressing from "Commercial Review" back to "Scoping" — and one rep was responsible for the majority of cases.

When they dug into the regression reason data, the explanation was straightforward: that rep was advancing deals to Commercial Review before the technical sign-off was complete. Procurement would push back, the deal would stall, and it would get kicked back to Scoping for a second round. Across affected deals, this habit was adding an average of 22 days to deal length. The fix wasn't a process overhaul — it was a stage gate rule requiring a completed technical sign-off field before Commercial Review became selectable, and a single coaching conversation.

Twenty-two days per deal, invisible until someone looked at the direction of movement rather than just the position.

How to Get Started

Start with your activity log today. Pick your ten longest open deals and check each one for backward stage movement. If you find regression in more than half, you have a systemic issue worth investigating properly.

Then build the dashboard view and make it part of your weekly pipeline review. The goal is to make backward movement visible — because right now, it's probably happening in silence.
