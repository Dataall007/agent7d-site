How to Auto-Generate a Service Level Agreement from Your monday.com Project Board

📅 November 16, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Your monday.com board already knows everything an SLA needs to say. It knows the client name, the scope, the response time commitments, who owns what, and when deliverables are due. And yet, most teams are still opening a Word doc, hunting down last quarter's SLA, and manually retyping all of that information — one field at a time — every time a new project kicks off.

That's not a document problem. That's a workflow problem.

The Problem

Manually drafting SLAs from board data leads to two things: wasted time and inconsistent agreements. When you're pulling values from monday.com into a Word document by hand, you introduce risk at every step. Response times get copied incorrectly. Escalation contacts are outdated. A clause that was updated in your internal template never makes it into the client-facing version.

For teams onboarding multiple clients per quarter, this compounds fast. Each bespoke SLA becomes its own maintenance burden, and no two documents quite match — which creates problems the moment a client actually needs to reference one.

There's also a subtler issue: what most businesses forget to include. A solid SLA doesn't just define uptime or ticket response windows. It should cover:

  • Escalation paths — who gets contacted if a first-line response fails, and in what timeframe
  • Exclusions — what falls outside the agreement (third-party outages, client-side delays)
  • Review and amendment procedures — how terms are updated when scope changes
  • Remedies and penalties — what actually happens if SLA terms are breached

These clauses don't live in anyone's project board by accident. They need to be built into your template logic from the start.

The Solution

The fix is to treat your monday.com board as the source of truth for SLA generation — not a reference you consult while typing in another application.

When your board already holds response time commitments, assigned owners, service tiers, and project timelines, a document generation tool can map those columns directly to the corresponding sections of your SLA template. The document gets produced the moment the board is ready, with no manual transcription required.

This is exactly what DocuAI does. It connects to your monday.com board, reads the item data you've already entered, and generates a fully formatted, client-ready SLA based on a template you define once. The right data goes into the right clause — every time.

Step by Step

1. Identify the monday.com columns that carry SLA content.

Typical candidates: Client Name, Service Tier, Initial Response Time, Resolution Target, Account Owner, Escalation Contact, Contract Start Date, Contract End Date, and any custom columns for scope or exclusions.

2. Build your SLA template with dynamic placeholders.

In DocuAI, you create a document template that mirrors your standard SLA structure. Each placeholder corresponds to a column from your board — {{response_time}}, {{escalation_contact}}, {{service_tier}}, and so on.

3. Map your columns to the template.

Inside DocuAI, connect each board column to its matching placeholder. This mapping is defined once and reused across every new client item or board.

4. Set a trigger.

You can configure DocuAI to generate the SLA automatically when a status column changes — for example, when "Onboarding Status" moves to "Agreement Pending." No manual initiation needed.

5. Review, finalize, and send.

The generated document lands in your monday.com item as an attachment or is sent directly for e-signature, depending on your workflow setup.

When SLA terms change mid-project: Update the relevant columns in your board, re-run the generation with an amendment template, and you have a documented revision without digging through email threads to find the original.

A managed IT services provider onboarding 10–15 new clients per quarter was writing a bespoke SLA for each one by hand. Their project manager would open the client's monday.com board, note the response time tiers, copy the account owner's name, check the contract dates, and then manually enter all of it into a Word template. Each SLA took 45–60 minutes to produce and still required a review round because values were occasionally transposed or pulled from the wrong item.

After implementing DocuAI, the SLA is generated automatically when the client board reaches the "Agreement Pending" stage. The document is accurate by default because it reads directly from the board — the same board the team is already maintaining. What took an hour now takes seconds, and the review round exists to check the scope, not to fix typos.

How to Get Started

Map your existing SLA structure to your monday.com columns. Identify every piece of information in your current agreement that already exists somewhere in your board — you'll likely find it covers 80–90% of the document. Build your template around that, add your standard boilerplate for the clauses that don't change, and let the board fill in the rest.
