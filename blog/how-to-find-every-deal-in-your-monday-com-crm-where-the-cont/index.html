How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Contact Person Has Never Been Logged

📅 September 20, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Deals without contact monday.com CRM audit — if you haven't run one, you're probably sitting on more of these than you think.

---

The Problem

A deal with no contact person is not a deal. It is a placeholder. There is no one to call, no one to email, no one to follow up with — just a company name sitting in a pipeline stage, aging quietly while your team assumes someone else owns it.

The uncomfortable truth is that most monday.com pipelines are full of these records. Not because sales teams are careless, but because contactless deals are easy to create and invisible until you go looking for them.

They arrive in three ways. First, rushed manual entry: a rep logs a conversation quickly between calls, drops in the company name, sets a deal value, and moves on — intending to add the contact later, but later never comes. Second, bulk imports: a CSV from a trade show, a webinar registration export, or a list from marketing lands in monday.com as a batch of new items. The company column gets filled. The contact column stays blank because the source data didn't have a clean name field, or nobody mapped it correctly during import. Third, deals created from email threads: someone forwards an email chain, a deal item gets spun up, and the contact field is populated with the sender domain or left entirely empty.

The result is a pipeline that looks healthy by deal count but is significantly less callable than the numbers suggest.

The Solution

Surface every deal where the contact person column is empty, triage the list by value and stage, enrich the records you can still save, and close the door on future contactless entries with column validation and automation.

This is not a multi-week data project. Done properly, a team of two can audit and remediate a pipeline of 200–300 deals in a focused afternoon. The filtering capability is already inside monday.com. You do not need additional software to find the problem — you just need to know where to look and what to do with what you find.

Step by Step

1. Filter your board for empty contact fields.

Open your deals board. Click Filter at the top of the board. Add a condition: your contact column — this is often a People column, a Text column, or a Connect Boards column depending on how your CRM is built — and set the condition to Is Empty. Apply the filter. Every item that surfaces is a contactless deal. Save this as a named view so you can return to it.

2. Add a Group By layer for pipeline stage.

With the filter active, click Group By and select your status or stage column. This immediately shows you whether your contactless deals are clustered in early stages (lower urgency) or sitting in Proposal Sent, Negotiation, or Closing (high urgency, fix immediately).

3. Sort by deal value descending.

Within each stage group, sort by your deal value column from highest to lowest. This gives you a prioritised repair list: high-value deals in late stages first, low-value deals in early stages last.

4. Add a Last Activity column if you don't have one.

If your board tracks last activity date, add it to this view. A deal worth £40,000 in Negotiation with no contact and no activity in 45 days is either dead or critically at risk. You need to know which before you can act.

5. Enrich the records.

For each deal on your priority list, check three places in this order: your email history (search the company name in your inbox — someone made contact at some point), LinkedIn (find the company, identify the most likely buyer persona based on the context of the deal), and any connected boards in monday.com where a contact record might already exist but wasn't linked. Update the contact field, log a note about the source, and set a follow-up task.

6. Prevent future contactless deals.

Go to your board settings and enable column validation on your contact column — set it as required before a deal can move past your first pipeline stage. Then create an automation: When an item is created and Contact is empty after 24 hours, notify the item owner. This catches anything that slips through at entry.

Real Example

A B2B SaaS company used monday.com to manage pipeline from a series of webinar campaigns. After each event, their marketing team exported the registration list and imported it directly into their deals board. The import mapped company name, email address, and deal source correctly — but the contact name field in monday.com was a People column linked to their internal user directory, not a plain text field. The webinar registrant names never made it across.

Eighty deals entered the pipeline this way across three events. The board looked active. Deal values had been estimated based on company size. But every single contact field was blank. The sales team had been following up on the company email addresses from the registration export — not from the CRM — which meant no activity was being logged and no one could tell from the board which deals had been touched.

When they ran a filter for empty contacts, 80 items appeared. Sorted by deal value, the top 12 represented over £600,000 in estimated pipeline. Six of them had not been contacted in over 30 days because the assigned rep assumed a colleague had handled it.

They spent one afternoon enriching the top 30 records using LinkedIn and their email history. Of those, 11 converted to booked meetings within two weeks. The remaining 50 were either disqualified or deprioritised with a task set for re-engagement.

The fix took less time than the original import.

How to Get Started

Open your monday.com deals board today and run the filter described above. Just look at the number. That number — the count of deals with no contact person — is the floor of what you are working with. From there, the triage and enrichment process is straightforward.

If you want a faster read on the full health of your pipeline — not just missing contacts, but stuck deals, unowned records, missing values, and stage distribution — there is a better starting point than a manual filter.
