How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Contact, Value, and Close Date Are All Filled In — But the Deal Has Never Once Been Touched

📅 October 15, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

A deal with a contact name, a dollar value, and a close date feels like a real deal. It shows up in your pipeline views. It counts toward your forecast. Someone probably feels good about it being there. But if no one has touched that record since the day it was created, it is not a deal — it is a placeholder wearing a deal's clothes.

This is one of the most common and most dangerous forms of pipeline rot, and monday.com gives you no automatic warning when it happens.

The Problem

Most pipeline hygiene advice focuses on records with obvious gaps — no value, no owner, no close date. Those are easy to spot. Incomplete records look broken, so people go fix them.

The records that actually damage your forecast are the ones that look fine. A deal with all the key fields filled in gets none of the scrutiny it deserves. It sits in your Proposal stage, or your Negotiation stage, and it continues to be counted in pipeline reports for weeks or months — sometimes quarters — after it stopped being a real opportunity.

The reason this pattern is so hard to catch in monday.com is structural. The platform does not surface inactivity as a signal. There is no built-in alert that says "this item has had zero column updates in 90 days." A well-filled record and a recently-worked record look identical in a standard board view. The only way to tell them apart is to deliberately look for the difference.

The Solution

The answer is to filter your board specifically for deals where surface completeness and actual activity have diverged. You are looking for records where the key data fields are populated, but the update log, column change history, and communication activity tell a different story — nothing has happened since creation.

Monday.com has the filtering tools to surface this pattern. You do not need a third-party integration to get started. What you need is to know which signals to check and how to layer them into a filter that isolates these records from everything else on your board.

Step by Step

Start by opening your CRM board and switching to a table view so you can see all columns clearly.

Step 1 — Filter for the fields that signal "complete." Create a filter group that shows only items where Contact is not blank, Deal Value is not blank, and Close Date is not blank. This gives you the universe of deals that look complete on paper.

Step 2 — Add a Last Updated column. If you do not already have one, go to the column center and add a Last Updated column. This auto-populates with the timestamp of the most recent change to any column on that item. Sort this column ascending so the oldest-untouched records rise to the top.

Step 3 — Set a threshold. Filter for Last Updated that is more than 30, 60, or 90 days ago — choose the threshold that matches your average sales cycle. Any deal that clears the completeness filter in Step 1 and the inactivity filter here is a candidate for immediate review.

Step 4 — Cross-reference the Updates tab. Click into each flagged deal and check the Updates section. Zero messages, no activity log entries, and no email thread mean the deal has been untouched since creation. A Last Updated timestamp can sometimes change from a backend sync — the Updates tab confirms whether a human has ever actually engaged with the record.

Step 5 — Check the Close Date. If the close date is already in the past, the deal is not just inactive — it is overdue and still sitting in your active pipeline. Flag these separately. They need to be closed lost or rescheduled immediately.

Real Example

A SaaS sales team using monday.com ran this exact filter during a pipeline review. They found 11 deals sitting in their Proposal stage, all with a contact, a value, and a close date filled in. Every one of those close dates had already passed — some by more than three months. Not a single column had been updated since the deals were created. No notes, no emails logged, no status changes.

All 11 were still being counted in the team's quarterly forecast.

The total pipeline value tied up in those records was over $280,000. None of it was real. The team had been reporting against a number that did not exist. Once they worked through each record — re-qualifying three, handing off two to a different rep, and closing eight as lost — their forecast shrank significantly but became something they could actually trust. That accuracy then let them prioritize the deals that were genuinely active and close two of them before quarter end.

How to Get Started

Run the filter sequence above on your board this week. Sort by Last Updated, set your inactivity threshold, and see what surfaces. Pay particular attention to anything in a late-stage bucket — Proposal, Negotiation, Contract Sent — because those deals carry the highest dollar values and have the most damage potential if they are phantom records.

If you want to see the full picture across your entire pipeline — not just the untouched deals, but all the patterns of field gaps, unowned records, stuck stages, and forecast inflation — a manual filter-by-filter audit takes hours and still misses things.
