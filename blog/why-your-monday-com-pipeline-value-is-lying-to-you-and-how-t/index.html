Why Your monday.com Pipeline Value Is Lying to You — and How to Find the Real Number

📅 September 2, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Your monday.com pipeline total is probably wrong. Not by a little — by a lot. The number sitting at the top of your board looks authoritative, but it's almost certainly inflated by deals that haven't moved in months, records with no owner, close dates that passed quietly without anyone updating them, and value fields that were never filled in at all. None of that stops monday.com from adding those deals into your rollup column.

This post will show you exactly how to strip the noise and calculate what your pipeline is actually worth.

---

The Problem

Most monday.com sales boards are built to capture deals, not to maintain them. Items get created when a lead comes in, a value gets typed in optimistically, a close date gets picked — often arbitrarily — and then the deal moves at its own pace while the board fields stay frozen in time.

The result is what I call phantom pipeline: dollar value that appears real in your summary row but has no realistic chance of converting. There are four types:

1. Stale deals — items with no activity logged in 60+ days. The deal may be dead, paused, or simply forgotten. The board doesn't know.

2. Overdue close dates — close dates that have already passed without being updated. These are the single biggest distortion. A deal dated Q1 sitting in your Q2 pipeline still contributes its full value to the rollup.

3. Missing or placeholder values — deals with a $0, blank, or "TBD" in the deal value column. monday.com's sum formula either ignores these silently or treats them as zero, depending on how your column is configured — but either way, you don't know what you're actually looking at.

4. Unowned records — deals with no assigned rep. If no one owns it, no one is working it. Unowned deals are almost always dead pipeline.

---

The Solution

The fix is a pipeline integrity filter — a view within monday.com that excludes any item failing basic data hygiene. You're not deleting deals. You're separating signal from noise so your reported number reflects reality.

A clean pipeline integrity view filters out any deal that meets one or more of these conditions:

  • Close date is in the past and hasn't been updated in the last 7 days
  • Last activity date is more than 45–60 days ago
  • Deal value is blank, $0, or below your minimum deal threshold
  • Owner column is empty

The value rollup on this filtered view is your real pipeline number.

You can go a step further and build a confidence-weighted pipeline figure. Create a formula column that multiplies deal value by a stage-based probability: {Deal Value} * {Stage Probability}. Sum that column across only the integrity-filtered view. That's the number worth reporting to leadership.

---

Step by Step

  • Add a "Last Activity Date" date column to your board if you don't have one. Update it whenever a meaningful action is logged.
  • Create a new view called "Pipeline Integrity." Apply filters: close date is not in the past, last activity is within 60 days, deal value is not blank and not zero, owner is not empty.
  • Check the sum of your deal value column in this filtered view. Compare it to your unfiltered total.
  • Add a "Stage Probability %" number column with values that match your sales stages (e.g., Proposal = 40%, Negotiation = 70%).
  • Add a formula column: {Deal Value} * ({Stage Probability} / 100). Sum this column in your integrity view. This is your weighted pipeline.
  • Review the gap between your raw total and your weighted integrity total weekly, not monthly.

    • ---

    Real Example

    A SaaS sales team was reporting $2.1M in pipeline to leadership each month. When we ran a column-by-column audit, $600K of that number was attached to deals with no activity in over 60 days, missing contact owners, or close dates that had already passed without being updated. Their real, workable pipeline was $1.5M — a 29% overstatement that was quietly warping their hiring decisions, their quota planning, and their forecast confidence.

    None of this was visible from the board's default view. The total looked fine. It wasn't.
