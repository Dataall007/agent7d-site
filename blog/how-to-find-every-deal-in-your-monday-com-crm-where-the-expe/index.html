How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Expected Revenue and the Quoted Amount Don't Match

📅 October 3, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

When the number in your Deal Value field doesn't match the number in the proposal you sent, your forecast is wrong before the quarter even starts. This is one of the most common — and most invisible — sources of revenue leakage in a monday.com CRM. Nobody catches it because nobody is explicitly looking for it.

Here's how to find every mismatch across your pipeline, and how to fix it systematically.

---

The Problem

Most monday.com CRM setups have at least two places where a deal's monetary value lives: a Deal Value (or Expected Revenue) column that feeds your pipeline dashboards, and the actual quoted amount — whether that's stored in a separate column, linked to a quote document, or sitting in a file attached to the item.

These two numbers should be identical. In practice, they drift constantly.

A rep updates the proposal after negotiation but forgets to update the board. A discount gets applied in the quote but the original number stays in monday.com. A deal gets split into phases and only the first phase gets logged. The result is a pipeline dashboard that confidently displays a number your finance team cannot reconcile with anything in the real world.

By the time you realize your Q3 forecast is off by $180,000, the quarter is already over.

---

The Solution

The fix is straightforward in principle: create a direct comparison between your Deal Value column and your Quoted Amount column on every open deal, and surface every record where those two numbers don't match.

The challenge is that most boards weren't set up with this comparison in mind. If your quoted amount isn't stored as a structured column — if it lives in a text note, an attached PDF, or a linked HubSpot record — you have to do some architecture work before you can automate the comparison.

Done right, this becomes a standing hygiene check that flags mismatches automatically, before they corrupt your forecast.

---

Step by Step

1. Standardize where the quoted amount lives.

If you don't already have a dedicated Numbers column for quoted amount, add one now. Call it "Quoted Amount" or "Proposal Value." Every rep should enter the exact figure from the most recently sent proposal here.

2. Add a formula column to calculate the variance.

monday.com's Formula column lets you subtract one number from another. Create a formula: {Quoted Amount} - {Deal Value}. Name it "Value Variance."

3. Add a second formula to flag mismatches.

Add a Status or Formula column that marks any record where the variance is not zero — or where either field is blank. A simple IF({Value Variance} = 0, "Match", "Mismatch") formula works well here.

4. Build a filter view called "Mismatches."

Filter your board to show only items where the mismatch flag is active. Save this as a named view. This is now your weekly reconciliation queue.

5. Set up an automation to notify the deal owner.

Use monday.com's native automations: when the Mismatch column changes to "Mismatch," notify the item's owner and ask them to reconcile the values. This removes the manager from having to chase individually.

6. Run the reconciliation weekly, not quarterly.

Weekly is the rhythm that actually prevents forecast corruption. Quarterly is cleanup after the damage is done.

---

Real Example

A SaaS sales team with 11 reps and roughly 200 open deals at any given time ran this exercise for the first time. They found 34 deals where the Deal Value and Quoted Amount didn't match. The total variance was $312,000 — some deals were overvalued in the CRM (proposals had been discounted), some were undervalued (upsells had been added to quotes but never updated in monday.com).

Their quarterly forecast had been built entirely from the Deal Value column. The actual expected revenue, based on real proposals in the field, was $218,000 lower. That's a meaningful miss — and it was entirely preventable with a two-column comparison.
