How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Stage and the Probability Don't Match — and Why It's Warping Your Forecast

📅 October 5, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Your forecast is only as accurate as the data feeding it. And one of the most common — and most invisible — sources of forecast rot in monday.com pipelines is the stage-probability mismatch: deals sitting in an advanced stage while still carrying the probability of a deal from two months ago.

No one flags it. No one fixes it. And then the quarter closes badly and everyone wonders why.

---

The Problem

Stage and probability are supposed to tell the same story from two different angles. Stage describes where a deal is in your process. Probability describes how likely it is to close. In a healthy pipeline, they move together.

In most pipelines, they don't.

A rep moves a deal from "Discovery" to "Proposal Sent" in monday.com but forgets — or never thinks — to update the probability field. The deal now lives in a late stage but still carries a 10% or 20% probability from wherever it started. Your weighted pipeline calculation picks up the stage for reporting and the probability for the weighted value, and suddenly you're looking at numbers that are internally contradictory and externally misleading.

The deeper problem: this is a silent error. monday.com won't warn you. Your dashboard won't turn red. The number just sits there, quietly lying.

---

The Solution

Surface every deal where the stage and probability are out of sync, fix the data, and build a guardrail that stops the mismatch from recurring.

This requires three things: a formula column that flags mismatches automatically, a triage process for the flagged deals, and an automation that enforces consistency going forward. All three are buildable directly inside monday.com — no external tools required.

---

Step by Step

1. Define your stage-to-probability map.

Before you can flag mismatches, you need to decide what the correct probability is for each stage. Write it down explicitly. For example: Discovery = 10–20%, Proposal Sent = 30–50%, Contract Sent = 70–85%, Verbal Commit = 90%. The exact numbers are less important than having a defined range per stage.

2. Build a formula column to flag mismatches.

In your monday.com board, add a Formula column. Use an IF statement that checks whether the probability in the Probability column falls within the acceptable range for the current stage. For a deal in "Contract Sent" that should be between 70–85%, a formula like IF({Stage} = "Contract Sent" AND {Probability} < 70, "⚠️ Mismatch", "OK") will immediately surface every outlier. Build one condition per stage, chained with AND/OR logic. Name the column something your reps will notice: "Forecast Integrity" works well.

3. Filter your board to show only flagged deals.

Use monday.com's filter function to isolate every item where your new column shows "Mismatch." This is your triage list. Export it or share it as a saved view with your sales manager before every pipeline review.

4. Add an automation to prevent future drift.

Go to Automations and create a trigger: When Stage changes, set Probability to [value]. Build one automation per stage transition. This enforces your stage-probability map at the moment of update — before the mismatch can happen — rather than catching it after the fact.

---

Real Example

A 12-person SaaS sales team ran this exercise across their monday.com board and found 23 deals sitting in "Contract Sent" that still showed 20% probability — the probability they'd been assigned when they first entered the pipeline, two months earlier. No one had updated them as the deals progressed.

The math was brutal. Those 23 deals had an average value of $73,500. At the correct 80% probability for their stage, they should have been contributing roughly $1.35 million to the weighted pipeline. At 20%, they were showing up as $340,000. The VP of Sales had been working from a committed pipeline number that understated reality by more than a million dollars — which meant her board forecast was built on a foundation that didn't reflect where the deals actually were.

When she presented to the board, the disconnect between her forecast and her close rate was impossible to explain. The deals were real. The problem was that the probability data had never caught up to the stage data.

---

How to Get Started

Run the triage now, before your next pipeline review. Build the formula column today — it takes less than 20 minutes and you'll immediately see the shape of your mismatch problem. Then build the automations so you're not running this exercise manually every quarter.

monday.com pipeline forecast accuracy doesn't come from working harder. It comes from trusting your data — and your data only becomes trustworthy when you build the right checks directly into the board.
