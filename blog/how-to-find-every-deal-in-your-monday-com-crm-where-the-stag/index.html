How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Stage Age Is Longer Than Your Average Sales Cycle — and What to Do Before They Expire

📅 October 30, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Most sales teams watch their pipeline by close date. That's the wrong signal. A deal can have a close date three weeks out and still be completely dead — because nobody touched it in six weeks. The metric that actually tells you whether a deal is alive is stage age: how long it's been sitting in its current pipeline stage.

If that number exceeds your team's average time in that stage, the deal isn't progressing. It's decaying.

The Problem

Deals stuck in pipeline monday.com users create are rarely flagged until they're already lost. There's no built-in alarm that says "this deal has been in Proposal Sent for 47 days and your average is 22." Instead, the deal just sits there, looking active in your count, inflating your pipeline value, and quietly rotting.

The danger isn't just lost revenue. It's false confidence. When your pipeline shows $400K in open deals and 35% of them are stalled, your forecast is fiction. You're making hiring, spending, and resourcing decisions on numbers that don't reflect reality.

Stage age is the honest signal. It doesn't care about the close date someone typed in optimistically six weeks ago. It measures actual elapsed time against actual historical performance. That gap is where the leakage lives.

The Solution

You need to calculate your average time-per-stage from real closed deals, compare that benchmark against your currently open deals, and surface every item where the current stage age has already exceeded it. Then you take action — not someday, but this week.

Monday.com gives you everything you need to do this. The data is in your boards. Most teams just haven't built the view that makes it visible.

Step by Step

1. Pull your average time-per-stage from closed deals.

Filter your CRM board to show only deals with a status of Closed Won or Closed Lost from the last 6–12 months. For each stage in your pipeline, calculate the average number of days deals spent there before moving forward. You can do this manually in a spreadsheet export, or use monday.com's dashboards with a Date column tracking when each stage was entered. This becomes your benchmark per stage.

2. Add a "Stage Entry Date" column if you don't have one.

This is a Date column that your team (or an automation) updates every time a deal moves into a new stage. If you're not tracking this yet, start today — you can't measure what you don't record. Create an automation: When Status changes, set Stage Entry Date to today.

3. Add a formula column for stage age.

Use a Formula column to calculate the difference between today and the Stage Entry Date. Monday.com's formula column supports TODAY() and date math, so something like TODAY() - {Stage Entry Date} gives you days elapsed. Call this column "Days in Stage."

4. Build a filtered view that flags overdue deals.

Create a new view on your CRM board. Filter by: current stage + Days in Stage greater than your benchmark for that stage. Save this as a recurring view your team checks every Monday morning. Color-code items that are over benchmark in red. Now the problem is visible.

5. For each flagged deal, run a 3-question triage.

Is there a next action logged? Is there an owner assigned? Is the key information (decision-maker, budget, timeline) actually captured? Nine times out of ten, one of these three is missing — and that absence is why the deal stalled.

Real Example

A SaaS sales team was reviewing their pipeline and noticed something uncomfortable. Of their 40 open deals, 14 were sitting in Proposal Sent. Their historical average time in that stage was 22 days. These 14 deals had all been there for 45 days or more — more than double the benchmark.

When they filtered further, every single one of those 14 deals had the same problem: no follow-up task logged. The proposals had gone out and been completely forgotten. No check-in email, no call scheduled, no activity on the record at all. The proposals were sent and then abandoned.

They recovered three of those deals by reaching out within the same week with a reframed message. Two required repricing to restart the conversation. Nine were moved out of the active pipeline entirely — cleaning up $180K in phantom pipeline value and giving the team a forecast they could actually trust.

The fix wasn't complex. It was visibility.

How to Get Started

Start with the Stage Entry Date automation if you don't have it — that's your foundation. Then pull six months of closed deals and calculate your average time per stage. Even rough numbers are more useful than none.

If your pipeline is more than 20 deals, doing this manually will surface things faster than you expect — and what you find is usually uncomfortable in a useful way.
