How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Stage Has Never Changed Since It Was Created

📅 October 24, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

There's a category of pipeline problem that most sales teams never think to look for: deals that didn't stall — they never moved at all.

A deal that goes backwards is visible. Someone notices. A deal that stays frozen in its opening stage from the moment it was created is invisible by design. It looks like a live lead. It counts toward your numbers. And it does exactly nothing.

Here's how to surface every one of them in your monday.com CRM, what they mean, and what to do about them.

---

The Problem

Most pipeline audits focus on deals that regress — moved to a later stage and slipped back. But the more damaging records are often the ones that registered as "created" and then simply... stopped.

A deal stuck in its first stage has never been qualified, never been advanced, and almost certainly never been worked. Despite that, it sits in your pipeline looking identical to a real opportunity. It inflates your deal count. It pads your pipeline value. And because it's not visibly declining, it doesn't trigger any of the normal warning signals your team watches for.

These records distort your forecast more than a lost deal does. A closed-lost deal is removed from the pipeline. A stage-frozen deal stays — permanently — making your numbers look stronger than they are.

---

The Solution

The fix requires two things: finding the records and understanding what caused them. In monday.com, you can isolate zero-movement deals using a combination of creation date filtering and stage-based sorting. Once you've pulled the list, each record falls into one of three categories — and each category has a different right action.

The goal isn't to delete everything. It's to stop letting dead or phantom records distort what your pipeline actually looks like.

---

Step by Step

1. Sort by creation date, filter by stage.

Open your CRM board in monday.com and apply a filter for your opening stage — whatever you call it: "New Lead," "Incoming," "Untouched." Then sort by creation date, oldest first. Any record that's been sitting there for more than 30 days without stage movement is a candidate for review.

2. Add a "Last Updated" column if you don't have one.

monday.com tracks last activity natively. Add a Last Updated column and sort by it. If a record's last update matches its creation date — nothing has ever been touched — that's a zero-movement deal.

3. Cross-reference with activity.

Use monday.com's Activity Log or any connected email/call integration to check whether any outreach was logged. A deal in "New Lead" with zero logged activity is not a pipeline item. It's a data entry artifact.

4. Tag each record before you action it.

Create a temporary Status column with three values: Duplicate, Dead Lead, Needs Qualification. Categorize every frozen deal before you touch it. This prevents hasty deletions and makes the triage auditable.

5. Action each category cleanly.

Duplicates: merge or delete. Dead leads with no activity: close-lost with a note and a reason. Unqualified records with a real contact: assign an owner and set a follow-up deadline. If no one claims it in five business days, close it.

6. Set a recurring filter view.

Save a board view in monday.com called "Stage Frozen 30+" — filtered to opening stage, sorted by creation date, showing everything older than 30 days. Review it monthly. Make it someone's job.

---

Real Example

A SaaS sales team ran this exercise on their monday.com board and found 40 deals sitting in "New Lead" for more than 90 days. When they categorized them: 12 were duplicates created by a form integration that wasn't deduplicating on submission. 15 belonged to contacts who had gone dark before any activity was ever logged — they should have been closed months earlier. The remaining 13 had real contacts but had never been assigned to a rep or run through any qualification step.

Those 40 deals represented enough total value to make their monthly forecast look approximately 20% stronger than it actually was. Every pipeline review for the previous quarter had been built on numbers that included this phantom inventory.
