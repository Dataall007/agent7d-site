How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Stage Hasn't Moved in 30 Days — and Whether It's Stalled or Just Hidden

📅 October 29, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

The Problem

Deal count is a vanity metric. What actually predicts whether you close the quarter is stage velocity — how fast deals move through your pipeline and whether they're moving at all.

A monday.com pipeline full of deals looks healthy on the surface. But scroll past the dashboard summary and you'll often find dozens of items sitting in the same stage they've been in for six weeks. Some of those are fine — a negotiation that's grinding forward in email threads your CRM doesn't see, a deal waiting on a legal review the rep knows is coming. Others are quietly dead. Nobody's touched them. There's no next action. The prospect stopped responding in October and the rep hasn't marked it lost because losing feels worse than uncertainty.

The problem isn't that deals stall. That's inevitable. The problem is that you can't tell from a standard monday.com board view which stagnant deals still have a pulse and which are burning pipeline capacity that should go elsewhere. Without that distinction, you'll either waste time chasing losses or let real opportunities expire while your reps stay focused on easier calls.

The Solution

Stage velocity filtering. Specifically, surfacing every deal where the stage hasn't changed in 30 or more days, then reading the surrounding data to diagnose what's actually happening.

Monday.com doesn't surface this by default — but the data is there. The combination of Last Updated timestamps, owner assignment, activity logs, and linked tasks gives you everything you need to separate a deal that's progressing quietly from one that's been silently abandoned.

The goal isn't to kill deals faster. It's to make sure every deal in your pipeline has a clear next action, a real owner, and a realistic close date — or gets removed so your funnel reflects reality.

Step by Step

1. Create a filtered view for stage-age.

In your Deals board, add a filter on the Last Updated column set to "is before" — then choose a date 30 days ago. This immediately collapses your board to records that haven't been touched in a month. Save this as a named view: "30-Day Non-Movers."

2. Group by pipeline stage.

With that filter active, switch grouping to your Stage column. Now you can see exactly how many stagnant deals are in each stage — and which stages are accumulating the most rot.

3. Check three things for each deal.

For every flagged record, look at: (a) whether there's an open task or next-action item linked to the deal, (b) who the owner is — or whether the owner field is blank, and (c) what the Close Date says. These three data points tell you almost everything.

  • No next action: The deal has been handed off to hope. It needs a task created before it touches another week.
  • No owner or wrong owner: This is an ops failure, not a sales failure. Reassign immediately.
  • Close date in a past quarter: Either the deal was never updated honestly or the prospect has gone cold. Treat it as lost unless there's documented recent contact.

4. Build a weekly dashboard widget.

Create a new Dashboard in monday.com and add a Table widget pointed at your stagnant deals view. Set it to display Stage, Owner, Close Date, and Last Updated. Share this dashboard with your sales manager. Make reviewing it a 15-minute Monday morning ritual.

5. Decide: archive, reassign, or re-engage.

If there's no activity, no owner, and a blown close date — archive it and move on. If there's a good owner but no task — create the task together and set a 7-day follow-up. If the deal looks genuinely viable but quiet — send a breakup email. Responses to breakup emails close more deals than most people expect.

Real Example

A SaaS sales team ran a stage-age filter on their monday.com board mid-quarter and found 18 deals sitting in the Proposal Sent stage — all of them untouched for over 45 days.

When they dug in: none of the 18 had a follow-up task attached. Two had no owner assigned at all — they'd been created during a team transition and simply never claimed. Three had close dates from the previous quarter, meaning they'd been quietly rolled over without any real assessment.

Fourteen of those 18 deals were invisible to management because the board showed "18 deals in Proposal Sent" — which sounds like pipeline. It wasn't pipeline. It was noise with a dollar amount attached.

After filtering and reviewing, the team archived nine deals, reassigned two, and re-engaged seven with a direct email. Three of those seven responded within a week.

How to Get Started

Run the stage-age filter today — not at the end of the quarter. Build the view, group by stage, and spend one hour going through every deal that surfaces. That hour will almost certainly recover more revenue than the next five hours your team spends prospecting cold leads.
