How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Lead Source Has Never Been Filled In

📅 October 27, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

If you don't know where a deal came from, you can't know where to invest next. That sounds obvious. Yet most monday.com pipelines are quietly accumulating deals where the lead source field is empty, defaulted to a placeholder, or filled in with something useless like "Other." The result is that your marketing attribution is built on a foundation of gaps — and the decisions you make from it are worse than useless.

This post shows you exactly how to find every deal with missing lead source data in your monday.com CRM, why it matters more than most teams realize, and how to fix it systematically.

---

The Problem

Lead source is arguably the most important field in a CRM. It connects every deal to the channel that generated it — paid search, referral, outbound, organic, event, whatever your mix looks like. Without it, you can't calculate true channel ROI, you can't compare customer lifetime value by acquisition source, and you can't make an evidence-based argument for where next quarter's budget should go.

And yet lead source is consistently the most ignored field in most monday.com CRMs.

Why? A few reasons. Sales reps are focused on moving deals forward, not filling in administrative fields. If lead source isn't required at the point of creation, it gets skipped. If the dropdown options don't match how deals actually arrive, reps leave it blank rather than pick something wrong. And if no one is auditing the field, no one notices the gaps accumulating.

The real cost isn't data hygiene. It's budget decisions made on bad signal. When 40%, 50%, or 60% of your closed-won deals have no source attached, every channel report you produce is fiction.

---

The Solution

The fix has two parts: visibility and enforcement.

First, you need to see the full scope of the problem — every deal in your pipeline where lead source is blank, null, or defaulted. Most teams have no idea how many records this affects until they actually pull the filter. Second, you need a process to backfill what you can and enforce completion going forward so the gap stops growing.

monday.com gives you the tools to do both. The challenge is knowing exactly how to configure them.

---

Step by Step

1. Create a filtered view for blank lead source

Open your CRM board (or Deals board) and add a new filter. Set the condition to: Lead Source — is empty. Save this as a named view — something like "Missing Lead Source" — so your team can return to it regularly.

If your lead source field uses a dropdown and reps have been selecting a default like "Unknown" or "Other," add a second filter condition with OR logic: Lead Source — is — [your placeholder value]. This surfaces the disguised blanks alongside the true empties.

2. Check the count before you do anything else

Look at that number. If it's more than 15-20% of your total pipeline, you have a systemic problem, not a data entry slip. That threshold matters because it tells you whether the fix is a one-time cleanup or a process change.

3. Backfill intelligently

Sort the missing records by close date (most recent first) and assign them to the reps who owned each deal. Give them a two-week window to fill in source from memory, email history, or CRM notes. For older records, use what context you have: if the deal came in through a specific campaign period, cross-reference with your marketing calendar or ad platform data.

4. Make the field required going forward

monday.com doesn't natively enforce required fields in the same way some CRMs do, but you can get close. Use an automation: when a deal is created and lead source is empty, trigger a notification to the record owner asking them to fill it in. Set a board-level expectation in your team documentation that deals cannot move past the first stage without a source attached. Some teams use column permissions to make the field visible and prominent at creation.

5. Add lead source to your weekly pipeline review

If it's not in the room, it won't get filled in. Add a "Missing Lead Source" count to whatever dashboard you review weekly. Make the gap visible on a recurring basis and it will close.

---

Real Example

A professional services firm — running paid search, a referral programme, and an outbound SDR team — wanted to assess which channel was driving the most revenue. They pulled their closed-won deals from the past 18 months and ran the analysis.

The problem: 60% of those deals had no lead source recorded in monday.com.

The remaining 40% showed referral as the top channel. But since the majority of deals had no source, that finding was statistically meaningless. Paid search looked weak — but was it actually weak, or were half the paid deals just untagged? The SDR team's numbers looked modest — but the same question applied.

The result was that the leadership team made their next budget allocation based on gut feel, which is exactly what the CRM was supposed to prevent. They ended up cutting paid search spend, only to find out six months later — after a manual audit of their email history — that several of their highest-value clients had originally come in through paid campaigns that were never attributed.

The cost of missing lead source data in monday.com wasn't a data problem. It was a $200K+ misallocation of marketing budget.

---

How to Get Started

Before you fix anything, you need to know what you're dealing with. Run the lead source filter on your board right now and look at the number. Then check what percentage of your closed-won deals are in that view.

If it's under 10%, you have a manageable cleanup task. If it's over 30%, you have a systematic attribution problem that is actively distorting every channel decision you make.

Either way, lead source data is just one of the fields that quietly drains pipeline health. Stuck deals, unowned records, deals missing close dates — they all compound the same way.
