How to Create a Project Closure Report from monday.com Without Writing a Single Word

📅 September 3, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Every piece of data you need for a project closure report already exists in your monday.com board. The timeline, the budget figures, the deliverables list, the open items — it's all there. Yet most teams still spend hours manually copying that information into a Word document or a slide deck before they can send anything to a client.

That's not a monday.com problem. It's a documentation workflow problem. And it has a straightforward fix.

---

The Problem

Project closure reports are one of the most consistently skipped or delayed deliverables in professional services. Not because teams don't understand their value, but because writing one feels like starting from scratch at exactly the moment everyone wants to move on to the next job.

Consider a mid-sized construction management firm closing six to ten projects per month. Their current process: a project coordinator pulls up the board, opens a closure report template in Word, and manually transcribes scope items, milestone dates, budget actuals, punch list status, and lessons learned — field by field, row by row. That's roughly two hours per project. At eight projects a month, that's sixteen hours of a coordinator's time spent re-entering data that already exists somewhere else.

Delayed or missing closure documents also carry a real cost to client relationships. A closure report signals professionalism. It says: we tracked everything, we delivered what we promised, and here's the proof. When it doesn't arrive — or arrives weeks late — clients notice. It colors their memory of the entire engagement.

---

The Solution

The solution is to treat your monday.com board as the single source of truth it already is, and generate the closure report directly from it the moment a project wraps.

DocuAI is a monday.com app that maps your board columns to a pre-built document template, then generates a fully formatted closure report — complete with your branding — from the project item data. No copy-paste. No manual formatting. No blank page to stare at.

When the project status flips to Done, the document builds itself.

---

Step by Step

1. Identify what a good project closure report actually contains.

The core sections: scope delivered, planned vs. actual timeline, budget vs. actuals, open or outstanding items, and lessons learned. Every one of these has a natural home in your monday.com board — status columns, date columns, number columns, text fields, subitems.

2. Audit your board columns against those sections.

Map each section of your closure report to the column that holds that data. Scope delivered → your task or deliverable group. Timeline comparison → start date and end date columns vs. baseline dates. Budget vs. actuals → your budget and spend columns. Open items → subitems still marked In Progress or On Hold. Lessons learned → a long-text column you populate during the project (or at close).

3. Build your closure report template in DocuAI.

Using DocuAI's template editor, place dynamic tags wherever you want board data to appear. {{project_name}}, {{actual_end_date}}, {{total_budget}}, {{lessons_learned}} — each tag pulls directly from the corresponding column when the document generates.

4. Set the trigger.

Configure DocuAI to generate the report automatically when the project's Status column changes to Done. No one has to remember to run it.

5. Review and personalise before sending.

The generated document lands in your monday.com item as an attachment. Open it, add any context-specific commentary that isn't captured in board data — a paragraph about a particular challenge, a note of thanks to the client — and send.
