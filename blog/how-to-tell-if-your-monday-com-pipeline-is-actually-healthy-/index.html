How to Tell If Your monday.com Pipeline Is Actually Healthy — or Just Busy

📅 September 22, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

A large number in the pipeline total feels good. It shouldn't. A bloated monday.com pipeline is one of the most effective ways to hide a revenue problem — from your leadership team, from your investors, and from yourself. The deals are there. The board looks full. But nothing is actually moving.

If your team is closing less than your pipeline suggests you should be, the issue usually isn't effort. It's signal clarity. You're optimizing for a metric that doesn't tell you what you think it does.

---

The Problem

Deal count and total pipeline value are the two most common health indicators sales teams track — and they're both nearly useless on their own.

Here's why: both metrics measure inventory, not momentum. A deal sitting untouched for 90 days still contributes to your pipeline total. A contact with no logged call, no email, no activity of any kind still shows up in your deal count. A record with no owner, no close date, and an estimated value someone typed in on day one still inflates your forecasted revenue.

What you end up with is a pipeline that looks healthy by every headline metric and is quietly bleeding deals you had a real shot at winning.

The monday.com interface makes this worse, not better. Boards are visual and intuitive, which is exactly what makes it easy to feel like things are in order when they're not. Moving a deal to a new status column looks like progress. It isn't — not unless something actually happened with the buyer.

The five signs your monday.com pipeline is busy but broken:

  • Deals that change columns without logged buyer activity — status moves that reflect internal optimism, not external signals
  • Contacts with no interaction history — open deals where no call, email, or meeting is recorded
  • Values that haven't changed in 60+ days — suggesting no qualification has happened since the deal was created
  • Empty or unassigned owner fields — records nobody is actually responsible for
  • Missing close dates — deals that exist in a forecasting vacuum with no expected timeline

    • Any one of these is a warning. Three or more in the same record is a dead deal you're still counting.

    ---

    The Solution

    A monday.com pipeline health check isn't about auditing effort — it's about separating real buyer engagement from administrative activity. The goal is to identify, by board, which deals have genuine forward momentum and which ones are noise.

    A healthy pipeline has five non-negotiable data conditions at the record level: an assigned owner, a logged last-activity date within a reasonable window (typically 30 days for active deals), a close date, a deal value that has been updated post-qualification, and at least one documented interaction in the deal timeline.

    When all five are present, you can make decisions. When any of them are missing, the deal is a guess.

    ---

    Step by Step

    Here's how to run a manual pipeline health check inside monday.com using native features:

    1. Filter for last-activity date. Add a date column called "Last Activity" if you don't have one. Filter your board to show only deals where this date is older than 30 days. Everything surfaced needs immediate review.

    2. Sort by owner and look for blanks. Use the Sort function on your owner/assignee column. Unowned deals group at the top or bottom depending on your sort direction. Any record without an owner is effectively abandoned.

    3. Check close date coverage. Filter for empty close date fields. In a healthy pipeline, this column should never be empty on an open deal. If it is, you don't have a deal — you have a wish.

    4. Review deal value update history. Use column update history or activity logs to check when deal values were last edited. Values unchanged since creation often indicate deals that were never properly qualified.

    5. Spot column-hopping with no activity. Sort by status change date alongside last-activity date. If a deal moved columns recently but the last logged activity is weeks old, someone updated the board — not the deal.

    Work through each of these checks systematically. On a mid-sized board, this takes 45 minutes to an hour to do manually.

    ---

    Real Example

    A 12-person SaaS sales team came to us with a straightforward problem: their pipeline showed $480K in open deals, but quarterly revenue kept falling short of forecast. Leadership assumed the team needed to close harder. The data told a different story.

    When we ran a pipeline health check on their monday.com board, filtering for last-activity date and checking for empty owner columns, the picture changed fast. Of the $480K in reported pipeline:

    • 38% of deals had no activity logged in over 60 days
    • 14% had no assigned owner
    • A further 11% had no close date and deal values unchanged since the initial entry

    In total, roughly 60% of the reported pipeline was stale, unowned, or missing critical qualification data. The real pipeline — deals with an owner, recent activity, and a close date — was closer to $190K.

    The team wasn't underperforming. They were working off a wildly inaccurate picture of what was actually live. Once the board was cleaned and real deals were separated from ghost inventory, forecasting became reliable and the team could focus effort where it actually mattered.
