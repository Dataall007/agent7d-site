How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM That Has Been Sitting in "Proposal Sent" for More Than 30 Days — and What to Do About Each One

📅 September 25, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

The Problem

"Proposal Sent" feels like progress. You did the work, you sent the document, and now you're waiting. That waiting is exactly where deals go to die quietly — and most sales teams never notice until the quarter ends badly.

Here's the psychology: once a rep sends a proposal, the deal feels almost closed. It gets mentally filed under "likely wins." Nobody wants to poke it too hard and risk breaking the spell. So reps nudge it down the pipeline in their heads, log it optimistically in the forecast, and avoid the uncomfortable follow-up call that might bring bad news.

Meanwhile, the prospect has moved on. They're buried in other priorities, or they chose a competitor, or they're waiting for budget approval they'll never get. And in your monday.com CRM, that deal sits in "Proposal Sent" like a ghost — still haunting your pipeline, still inflating your numbers, still consuming mental bandwidth.

The monday.com proposal sent deals stuck problem is more common than most sales leaders want to admit. If your average deal cycle is 30–45 days and you have proposals sitting untouched at day 60, you're not managing a pipeline — you're managing a backlog of wishful thinking.

The Solution

Surface every stuck proposal systematically, categorize them fast, and assign a specific next action to each one today. Not next week. Today.

The good news is that monday.com gives you everything you need to do this in under 20 minutes — filtering, sorting, and automations that can flag stale deals before they even hit the 30-day mark.

Step by Step

1. Filter your board to isolate every "Proposal Sent" deal past 30 days.

Open your Deals board in monday.com. Click the Filter icon in the top toolbar. Set your first condition: Stage = "Proposal Sent." Add a second condition: Last Activity Date is before [today minus 30 days]. If you're using a Date column called "Proposal Sent Date," filter on that instead — it's more precise than activity date.

Save this as a named view ("Stale Proposals") so you can check it weekly without rebuilding the filter.

2. Sort by deal value, descending.

You want your highest-value stuck deals at the top. Don't waste your first hour of triage energy on a $2k deal when there's a $40k opportunity three rows down.

3. Categorize every deal into one of three buckets.

  • Genuinely pending: The prospect acknowledged receipt, asked for time, gave a specific revisit date. These are fine — log the next touchpoint and move on.
  • Silently dead: No response to the proposal, no response to at least two follow-ups, no activity in 45+ days. Mark these as Lost or move them to a "Cold" stage. Keeping them in "Proposal Sent" is lying to your forecast.
  • Needs a nudge: One follow-up sent, proposal acknowledged but no decision shared, prospect has gone quiet but context suggests they're still interested. These are your recoverable deals.

4. Assign specific follow-up actions based on deal value and last activity.

For "Needs a Nudge" deals above your average deal value: assign a personal call within 24 hours, not another email. For smaller deals in the same bucket: assign a direct, short email asking for a yes/no. Give them an easy out — it forces a real response.

5. Set up a monday.com automation to catch proposals going stale before 30 days hits.

Go to Automations → Custom Automation. Set the trigger: "When date arrives" (use your Proposal Sent Date column, set to 21 days after). Set the action: notify the deal owner and change a status column to "Follow-Up Overdue." This gives reps a 9-day runway to act before a deal officially becomes a problem.

Real Example

A SaaS account executive at a 15-person sales team ran this exact filter during a pipeline review. She found 11 proposals sitting untouched — the oldest at 62 days. Two were marked "Silently Dead" immediately and closed out as Lost. Six got a short "yes or no" email that afternoon. Three high-value deals ($8k–$14k range) got personal calls.

By end of week: two deals totaling $28k moved to Negotiation. One of them closed 12 days later. The prospect had simply been waiting for someone to follow up with authority.

The filter took her four minutes to build. The triage took 20 minutes. The follow-up sequence took one afternoon.

How to Get Started

Pull up your monday.com Deals board right now and run the filter. If you see more than five deals in "Proposal Sent" past 30 days, you have a pipeline hygiene problem worth fixing today.
