How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM That Has Been Sitting in the Same Stage for More Than 30 Days — and What to Do About Each One

📅 September 24, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

A stalled deal is not a neutral event. Every open opportunity that hasn't moved in 30 days is quietly corrupting three things at once: your forecast, your team's calendar, and your ability to know what's actually real in your pipeline. Here's how to surface every one of them — and what to do once you have.

---

The Problem

Most sales managers look at their monday.com pipeline by stage. They see deals in "Proposal Sent" or "Negotiation" and feel a rough sense of progress. What they don't see is time — specifically, how long each deal has been sitting in that stage without moving.

A deal that entered "Proposal Sent" yesterday and one that entered it 47 days ago look identical in a standard board view. They are not identical. One is live. One is almost certainly dead — and it's inflating your forecast, consuming follow-up effort, and giving your team a false sense of a full pipeline.

Stuck deals in a monday.com pipeline don't announce themselves. You have to go looking.

---

The Solution

The fix is a targeted filter that isolates every deal where the status column hasn't changed in more than 30 days. Combined with a few secondary filters, this gives you a triage list — not just a list of slow deals, but a list sorted by why they're slow, which is the only information that tells you what to do next.

The five reasons deals stall, in order of frequency:

  • Champion went quiet — your contact stopped responding and no one followed up
  • Budget was never confirmed — the deal moved forward on enthusiasm, not approval
  • Wrong rep assigned — the rep left, changed role, or was never the right fit for the account
  • Proposal was never actually sent — the stage was updated prematurely
  • Deal is already dead — everyone internally knows it, but no one closed it as lost

    • Each of these has a different fix. Which means your first job isn't to "follow up on stuck deals" — it's to diagnose which category each one belongs to.

    ---

    Step by Step

    1. Add a "Last Stage Change" date column if you don't have one. In monday.com, you can use a Date column and update it via automation: set a rule that triggers whenever your Status column changes, writing the current date into that field. If you already use a Last Updated column that captures status changes specifically, that works too.

    2. Filter by that date column. Use the Filter menu → select your Last Stage Change column → condition: "is before" → value: 30 days ago. This surfaces every deal where the stage has been frozen for a month or more.

    3. Add a secondary filter for stage if you want to isolate a specific part of the funnel. Filtering to "Proposal Sent" + no movement in 30 days is often the most revealing single query you can run.

    4. Sort by close date. Deals with a close date that's already passed are your most urgent triage cases.

    5. Cross-reference the Owner column. Look for deals assigned to people no longer on the team, or deals with no owner at all.

    You now have a prioritized list. Work from the bottom up — oldest and most overdue first.

    ---

    Real Example

    A sales manager runs this filter on a board with 60 open deals. The result: 14 deals have been sitting in "Proposal Sent" for more than 30 days.

    On closer inspection: six have no last-contact date logged anywhere in the item. Two are assigned to a rep who left the company six weeks ago. Three have a close date from three months ago that was never updated. And three of the remaining ones have activity — but the activity is internal notes, not actual client contact.

    None of this was visible before the filter ran. The pipeline looked healthy at 60 open deals. The reality was closer to 40 — and even within those 40, the data quality was poor enough to make the forecast unreliable.

    The manager closed out eight deals as lost that afternoon, reassigned two, and had clear action items on the remaining four. Forecast accuracy improved immediately — not because the pipeline got bigger, but because it got honest.
