How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM That Has Been Reopened More Than Once — and What That Pattern Is Telling You

📅 September 6, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

There is a category of deal that looks like progress but is actually a red flag. It gets marked Closed Won, then reopened a week later. Closed again. Reopened again. Each cycle generates activity, movement, and the appearance of a functioning pipeline. None of it is real.

Deals that are repeatedly reopened are one of the most reliable signs that something is broken in your sales process — and because they blend into normal pipeline activity, most teams never stop to name the problem.

The Problem

Reopening a deal once is normal. A decision slips, a budget cycle resets, a contact goes quiet and comes back. That happens. But when the same deal gets reopened two or three times, or when you find ten, fifteen, twenty deals across your pipeline sharing this pattern, you are no longer looking at a series of unfortunate events. You are looking at a process failure that is quietly corrupting your numbers.

The damage operates on two levels. First, your close rate looks better than it is. Every time a deal is marked closed, it registers as a conversion — even if it reopens forty-eight hours later. If you are calculating win rate from status changes, serial reopeners inflate it artificially. Second, your forecast accuracy deteriorates. Deals that have been reopened multiple times are fundamentally less predictable. They carry hidden risk that your pipeline report does not capture.

Most teams are measuring the wrong things. They track time in stage, deal age, and activity volume. Reopen frequency sits in the status history, ignored.

The Solution

The fix starts with visibility. You need to surface every deal in your monday.com CRM that has been reopened at least once — and flag those reopened more than once as priority investigations.

monday.com stores status change history at the item level. That history is your data source. Once you can see which deals have cycled through a closed status more than once, you can categorise the reopen reason, spot the patterns, and trace each pattern back to its upstream cause.

The goal is not to punish your team for reopening deals. The goal is to use reopen frequency as a diagnostic signal — a symptom that points to a specific process failure you can actually fix.

Step by Step

1. Access your status change history. In monday.com, open any deal item and navigate to the Updates section or the Activity Log. Every status change is timestamped and recorded. For a systematic review, use monday.com's activity export or connect your board to a reporting tool that can query status history at scale.

2. Build a reopen counter column. Create a formula column or use monday.com automations to flag any item where a closed status was followed by a non-closed status more than once. If you are doing this manually at first, export your board data and filter by items that have "Closed" appearing multiple times in the status history column.

3. Segment by reopen frequency. Separate deals reopened once from those reopened two or more times. The twice-plus group is your urgent list. Tag them with a dedicated label — "Serial Reopen" works — so they stay visible.

4. Add a Reopen Reason field. Create a dropdown column with options covering the most common causes: Pricing Not Confirmed, Legal or Compliance Hold, Stakeholder Change, Internal Approval Pending, Deal Closed Too Early. Have your reps populate this retrospectively for flagged deals.

5. Run the frequency analysis. Group your serial reopeners by reopen reason. You are looking for concentration. If 70% share the same reason, you have found your root cause.

6. Trace it upstream. Each reopen reason points to a specific stage in your process where a critical condition is not being verified before close. Identify that stage, add an explicit checklist item or required field, and update your exit criteria.

Real Example

A SaaS company reviewed their monday.com CRM at the end of a quarter and found 22 deals that had been reopened during the period. Seven of those had been reopened twice. The team had attributed most of them to normal deal complexity. The numbers told a different story.

When they categorised each deal by reopen reason, 18 of the 22 — roughly 80% — shared a single cause: the deal had been marked Closed Won before legal review was complete. The customer's legal team would surface objections post-signature request, the deal would stall, and the rep would reopen it rather than log a formal loss.

The pipeline impact was significant. Their reported Q3 win rate was 34%. Adjusted for serial reopeners, it was closer to 26%. Their average sales cycle, calculated from the original open date, was being artificially compressed because closed dates were being reset on reopen.

The fix was simple once the pattern was named: add a mandatory Legal Approval Confirmed checkbox to the monday.com board as a required field before any deal can move to Closed Won. Deals that do not have legal sign-off stay in a Pending Legal stage. The reopen problem did not disappear overnight, but new deals stopped entering the cycle.

How to Get Started

Pull your monday.com board activity log for the last 90 days. Sort by deal items. Look for any item where a closed status appears more than once in the history. Count them. If you find more than five, you have enough to start categorising.

The categorisation step is what most teams skip. Finding the deals is the easy part. Understanding why they keep cycling — and naming the upstream failure each reason points to — is where the real work happens. Start there.
