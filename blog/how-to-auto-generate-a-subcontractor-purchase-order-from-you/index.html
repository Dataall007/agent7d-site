How to Auto-Generate a Subcontractor Purchase Order from Your monday.com Project Board

📅 September 19, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

If you manage vendor relationships inside monday.com, you already know the pattern: the data is right there on the board — vendor name, line item, quantity, unit cost, delivery date — and yet somehow the purchase order still gets created somewhere else. In a spreadsheet. In Word. In a PDF copy of last month's version that someone found in a shared drive folder called "FINAL_v3."

This post walks through how to break that cycle and generate a complete, accurate PO the moment a budget line is confirmed on your board.

---

The Problem

monday.com boards contain everything a purchase order needs. The vendor is in a People or text column. The item description lives in the task name or a column. Quantities, unit costs, and delivery dates all have natural homes in Number and Date columns. The approval status is right there in a Status column.

So why does the PO still get created outside monday.com?

Because monday.com does not natively produce a formatted output document. It stores data beautifully, but it does not hand you a PDF you can email to a subcontractor. So users do what they have always done: they export the board to Excel, reformat the rows into something that looks like a PO, save it as a PDF, and send it. Then they do it again next week. And the week after that.

The cost is real. Every manual transfer introduces copy-paste errors — wrong quantities, outdated unit prices, a vendor name that is subtly misspelled from the version two months ago. For operations teams issuing dozens of POs per project, these errors compound fast.

---

The Solution

The fix is to treat your monday.com board as the single source of truth and generate the PO directly from it — no export, no reformatting, no copy-paste.

When a board item triggers document generation, the data flows straight from the column into the corresponding PO field. Vendor name maps to the "Supplier" line. The Number column maps to "Unit Cost." The Date column maps to "Required Delivery Date." The output is a formatted, professional document that reflects exactly what is on the board at the moment it is generated.

This is what DocuAI does for monday.com. It connects to your board, reads the relevant columns, populates a PO template you define, and produces a ready-to-send document — triggered by a status change, a date, or a manual action, depending on how your workflow is structured.

---

Step by Step

1. Audit your board columns against standard PO fields.

Most POs require: vendor name, PO number, line item description, quantity, unit cost, total cost, delivery date, and payment terms. Map each of these to an existing monday.com column. If a column is missing, add it now — this is the moment to make your board the authoritative record.

2. Build your PO template.

Create a document template with placeholder fields that correspond to your column names. DocuAI uses these placeholders to inject live board data when the document is generated. Keep the template clean and professional — it is the document your subcontractors will receive.

3. Define your trigger.

Decide what confirms a budget line: a Status column changing to "Approved," a checkbox being ticked, or a specific date arriving. That event becomes the trigger for PO generation. Automating off a Status change is the most reliable option for most operations teams.

4. Test with a single item.

Before rolling out to the full board, generate a PO from one confirmed line item. Check that every field populated correctly. Verify the formatting. Send it to yourself and open it as your subcontractor would.

5. Deploy and document the process.

Once the template is confirmed, roll it out. Document the trigger and the expected output so any team member can use it without guidance.

  • Open your monday.com board and match each field to a column. Note any gaps.
  • Fill the gaps by adding columns to your board — do not compromise the document structure to fit a limited board setup.
  • Install DocuAI from the monday.com marketplace and connect it to your board.
