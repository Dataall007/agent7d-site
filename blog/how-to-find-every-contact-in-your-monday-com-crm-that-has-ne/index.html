How to Find Every Contact in Your monday.com CRM That Has Never Been Contacted — and What to Do With Them

📅 August 31, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Most monday.com pipelines have a graveyard hiding in plain sight. Contacts were added, assigned to a stage, and then nothing. No call logged. No email sent. No next-action date. They sit there, slowly going cold, while your team focuses on the deals already in motion.

This is not a laziness problem. It is a visibility problem. Here is how to fix it.

---

The Problem

Uncontacted leads in a monday.com CRM accumulate for three predictable reasons.

First, intake is faster than capacity. Inbound enquiries get added — from web forms, referrals, direct calls — but there is no enforced handoff. The lead exists in the board. No one owns the next step.

Second, ownership is ambiguous. A contact is assigned to a rep who is overloaded, on holiday, or no longer with the company. The record stays in the pipeline but effectively belongs to no one.

Third, there is no visibility mechanism. If your board does not surface "zero activity" contacts visually, you will never naturally encounter them. The pipeline view shows movement and urgency. Dormant records are invisible until someone goes looking — and without a process, no one goes looking.

The result is predictable. A meaningful percentage of your pipeline represents real revenue that has simply been abandoned by accident.

---

The Solution

Two columns make "never contacted" visible without any automation or paid add-ons.

Column one: Last Activity Date. This is a Date column that your team updates — or that you populate via automation — every time a meaningful touchpoint occurs. A call, an email reply, a meeting. If this column is blank or more than 30 days old, that contact has gone quiet.

Column two: Next Action Date. Also a Date column. This represents the committed next step. If it is blank, there is no follow-up scheduled. If it is in the past, it was missed.

Together, these two columns create a binary test: a contact with no Last Activity Date and no Next Action Date has never been meaningfully touched. That is your uncontacted leads list.

---

Step by Step

Here is the filter recipe to build a zero-activity view in monday.com right now.

  • Open your CRM board and click Filter in the top toolbar.
  • Add a filter: Last Activity Date → is empty.
  • Add a second filter: Next Action Date → is empty.
  • Set the filter logic to AND so both conditions must be true simultaneously.
  • Save this as a new View — name it something direct like "Never Contacted".
  • Add a secondary sort by Date Added (ascending) so the oldest uncontacted records surface first.

    • You now have a permanent, live view that shows every contact with no logged activity and no scheduled follow-up. Bookmark it. Review it weekly.

    To age the list, add a Formula column that calculates days since the record was created. Contacts under 30 days are recoverable with a simple outreach sequence. Contacts over 90 days need a different approach entirely — the context has changed, and your opening message needs to acknowledge the gap honestly rather than pretending time has not passed.

    ---

    Real Example

    A commercial real estate broker was pulling inbound enquiries from three sources into monday.com: a web form, referrals from two partner firms, and direct calls logged manually by the front desk. The pipeline looked active. Deals were moving.

    When they built the Never Contacted view using the method above, the number stopped them cold. Thirty-four percent of contacts in the CRM had no activity logged and no next-action date set. Some were three weeks old. Several were from the previous quarter.

    The triage framework they applied was straightforward. Contacts added within 30 days: immediate personal outreach with a specific, relevant opener tied to their original enquiry. Contacts between 30 and 90 days: a re-engagement sequence acknowledging the delay, offering something concrete. Contacts over 90 days: a one-time honest re-engagement attempt, and if no response within two weeks, archived with a tag so the record stays clean but retrievable.

    Within six weeks, they had converted three deals directly from that dormant list. One had been sitting untouched for 67 days.
