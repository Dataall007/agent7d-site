How to Spot the Deals in Your monday.com Pipeline Where You're the Only One Talking

📅 September 8, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Target keyword: monday.com one-sided deals pipeline audit

---

The Problem

There's a category of deal in almost every monday.com pipeline that's more dangerous than a lost deal. At least a lost deal is honest. This one sits in your board looking healthy — it has a stage, a value, a close date — but if you look closely at the activity log, every single touchpoint was initiated by you.

You sent the follow-up. You booked the call. You sent the proposal. You followed up on the proposal. The prospect hasn't replied in six weeks.

That deal is almost certainly dead. But it doesn't show up that way in your forecast. It shows up as a $40,000 opportunity at 60% probability, and your revenue projection treats it like real money.

One-sided deals — where all logged activity is outbound and inbound engagement has flatlined — are a structural forecasting problem. They accumulate quietly. They inflate pipeline value. And when they finally collapse, usually with no formal close, they vanish from your board without ever being counted as losses — which means you never learn from them.

---

The Solution

The fix is a systematic one-sided deal audit built directly inside monday.com, using the activity and date columns you likely already have (or should have) in your CRM board.

The goal is simple: surface every deal where inbound activity — replies, meeting requests, document opens, inbound calls — hasn't been logged in a meaningful window, typically 30 to 45 days. Then triage those deals deliberately: re-engage the ones worth chasing, and formally disqualify the ones that are already gone.

This isn't about being pessimistic about your pipeline. It's about making your forecast usable.

---

Step by Step

1. Add a "Last Inbound Activity" date column to your monday.com board.

This is separate from "Last Contact," which most teams use for any activity. You want a column that only gets updated when the prospect initiates something — a reply, a callback, a meeting request. If you've been tracking this in notes but not in a structured column, now is the time to fix it.

2. Add a "Inbound Activity Count" number column.

Log every inbound touchpoint as a tally. A deal with zero inbound activity logged is an immediate flag.

3. Filter your board for open deals where "Last Inbound Activity" is blank or older than 45 days.

In monday.com, use the Filter function at the top of your board. Set conditions: Stage is not "Closed Won" or "Closed Lost," and Last Inbound Activity date is empty or before your threshold date.

4. Sort by deal value descending.

You want to review the highest-stakes one-sided deals first.

5. For each flagged deal, check the Updates section.

Look at who initiated every conversation thread. If it's exclusively your team, that's a one-sided deal. If there's even one recent inbound message, downgrade the severity.

6. Label each flagged deal with a custom status column: "Re-Engage," "Final Attempt," or "Disqualify."

This gives you a clear action queue rather than a list of problems with no next step.

---

Real Example

A B2B services firm using monday.com had 40 open deals across two account executives. When they filtered for deals with no logged inbound activity in 45-plus days, 14 deals surfaced — representing roughly $380,000 in pipeline value.

They reviewed each one by checking the Updates section and their email integration logs. Here's what they found:

  • 4 deals had genuine recent context — the contact was at a conference, a contract was being reviewed internally, there was a documented reason for silence. These stayed open.
  • 6 deals were borderline — last real engagement was 30–50 days ago, no clear reason for silence. These moved to "Final Attempt" status with a 7-day deadline for a re-engagement response.
  • 4 deals had no inbound contact in 60-plus days, no reply to multiple follow-ups, and no documented reason. These were formally disqualified.

The result: their pipeline dropped from $380,000 to roughly $210,000 for those 14 deals — but their forecast accuracy improved immediately. The four disqualified deals were closed as Lost with a disqualification reason logged, giving them data they could actually use.

---

How to Get Started

Start with the filter I described above — Last Inbound Activity blank or older than 45 days, status not closed. If you haven't been tracking inbound activity separately, use the Updates section as a proxy and manually review the last five touchpoints for each flagged deal.

Do this once a month. It takes less than an hour for most pipelines and removes the single biggest source of forecast distortion in monday.com CRM boards.
