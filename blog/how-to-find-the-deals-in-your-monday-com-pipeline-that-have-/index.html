How to Find the Deals in Your monday.com Pipeline That Have Changed Stage But Never Changed Value

📅 September 16, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

There is a specific kind of broken deal that is almost impossible to spot by looking at your pipeline board. It looks active. It has a stage. It has a value. And that value is almost certainly wrong.

These are deals where someone moved the stage column forward — sometimes multiple times — but never touched the deal value after the initial entry at qualification. They are being managed by assumption, not by what the deal actually looks like today. And they are quietly destroying your forecast accuracy.

---

The Problem

When a deal enters your pipeline, the value entered at that moment reflects your best guess based on what the prospect told you during discovery or qualification. That number is not a fact — it is a hypothesis.

As the deal progresses, scope changes. The prospect adds requirements, removes a product line, delays a phase, or restructures their budget. In a well-run sales process, the deal value is revised to reflect that reality. In most monday.com boards, it isn't.

The result is a weighted pipeline that looks healthy on paper and produces revenue that consistently comes in below forecast. The deals close — but they close smaller, or differently, than the pipeline suggested.

This is not a data entry problem. It is a process design problem. And it starts with deals that have moved through multiple stages while their value column has sat frozen since day one.

---

The Solution

The fix has two components. The first is surfacing the records that have this pattern right now — stage movement without value movement. The second is changing your board design and review cadence so the pattern stops recurring.

Monday.com stores column history on every item. That means you can reconstruct, for any deal, whether the stage column and the value column were updated at similar points in time. When you find a deal where the stage has been updated three times and the value column shows one single entry from the creation date, you have found a deal being managed by assumption.

---

Step by Step

1. Open your CRM board and enable the Activity Log view.

On any item, click the activity log icon (the clock symbol in the item's detail panel). This shows a timestamped history of every column change made to that record.

2. Look for the pattern: multiple stage updates, one value entry.

For a deal at Proposal or Negotiation stage, you should see the value column updated at least once after qualification — ideally after the scope was confirmed. If the value column shows only the original entry, that deal's number has never been questioned.

3. Build a filter to narrow your search at scale.

Use monday.com's filter panel on your pipeline board. Filter by stage — start with deals at Proposal, Negotiation, or Contract Sent. Then sort by creation date ascending. Any deal created more than 45 days ago that is sitting in a late stage is your first audit group. Pull these into a dashboard and manually check column history for each.

4. Add a "Value Last Reviewed" date column to your board.

This is the structural fix. Add a Date column labeled "Value Last Reviewed." Make it a required update whenever a rep moves a deal to a new stage. This creates a visible, filterable record of whether the value has been actively maintained or simply inherited.

5. Create an automation to flag stale values.

Use monday.com's automation center to build a rule: when Stage changes, and Value Last Reviewed is more than 30 days ago, notify the deal owner and their manager. This removes the dependency on anyone remembering to check.

---

Real Example

A VP of Sales at a mid-sized professional services firm noticed something consistent and frustrating: their weighted pipeline suggested they would close approximately $840,000 in Q3. Actual closed-won revenue came in at $610,000. This happened two quarters in a row.

When they audited their monday.com board, the pattern became clear. Fifteen deals in the Proposal and Negotiation stages had values set at qualification that had never been revised. Three of those deals had experienced significant scope reductions during the sales process — one prospect had deferred an entire implementation phase to the following year. The deal was still in the pipeline at its original value, weighted at 70%.

The "leak" was not lost deals. It was deals closing at 60–70% of their forecast value because the value column had never been updated when scope changed.

Once they added the Value Last Reviewed column and built the stage-change automation, forecast variance in the following quarter dropped from 27% to 11%.

---

How to Get Started

Start this week by pulling every deal currently in Proposal, Negotiation, or Contract Sent that is more than 30 days old. Open the activity log on each one and check whether the value column has been updated since the deal was created.

What you find will likely be uncomfortable. Most teams discover that 40–60% of their late-stage deals are carrying values that have never been revisited. That is not a rep performance issue — it is a board design issue. Reps do not update values because nothing in the workflow asks them to.

Add the Value Last Reviewed column. Build the automation. Make value review a visible, required step at each stage transition — not an afterthought at the end of the quarter.

The goal is not to make your pipeline smaller. The goal is to make your forecast mean something.
