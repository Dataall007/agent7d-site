How to Generate a Board-Approved Meeting Agenda and Follow-Up Summary Directly from Your monday.com Items

📅 September 17, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

The Problem

Here is a scenario that plays out in project teams everywhere: the meeting is in 30 minutes, and someone is frantically copy-pasting task names, status labels, and owner names from a monday.com board into a Word document. Then, after the call, they spend another 45 minutes reformatting their notes into a follow-up summary and emailing it to the client.

The work itself took two hours. The documentation took almost as long.

For monday.com teams, this is one of the most persistent hidden time drains. The data you need for a professional meeting agenda and a clean follow-up summary already lives inside your board. The problem is that nothing connects it to a formatted, sendable document automatically. So the gap gets filled manually — every single time.

This is especially costly for project managers running recurring client calls. Every week, the same columns get read, the same information gets typed somewhere else, and the same formatting happens from scratch.

The Solution

Your monday.com board is not just a task tracker. If it is set up correctly, it is a structured data source that maps directly onto the components of a meeting agenda and a follow-up summary.

An agenda needs: topics to cover, owners for each topic, relevant status context, and time estimates. A follow-up summary needs: decisions made, action items, owners, and due dates.

Most boards already have columns that correspond to each of these — Item Name, Owner, Status, Due Date, Priority, and often a Notes or Comments field. The solution is to stop treating your board and your documents as two separate things and start generating the document directly from the board data.

Step by Step

1. Audit your board columns for agenda-worthy data.

Look for columns that answer these questions: What needs to be discussed? Who owns it? What is its current status? When is it due? If you have columns for Owner, Status, Due Date, and Priority, you already have the raw material.

2. Create a Meeting Agenda column group or filter view.

Use a filter or group to surface only the items relevant to the upcoming meeting — for example, items with a status of "In Progress" or "Blocked," or items tagged with a specific label like "Client Review." This becomes your agenda source.

3. Map board columns to agenda sections.

Item Name → Agenda Topic. Owner → Presenter. Status → Current State. Priority → Discussion urgency. Notes column → Context or pre-read. This mapping is the template logic that turns rows into agenda items.

4. After the meeting, update items in real time.

Add a "Decision" column and a "Follow-Up Action" column if you do not already have them. During or immediately after the call, log outcomes directly into the board. This is your follow-up summary data — already structured, already attributed.

5. Generate both documents from the same item data.

Using DocuAI, you can pull this structured data and produce a formatted agenda before the meeting and a formatted follow-up summary after it — both from the same board items, without retyping anything.

Real Example

A project manager at a mid-size construction firm runs weekly progress calls with three separate clients. Before each call, she manually copies task names, completion percentages, and blockers from her monday.com board into a Word template. After each call, she rewrites the discussion outcomes into a separate email.

After restructuring her board to include a "Discussion Point" checkbox column and a "Post-Call Action" text column, she was able to use DocuAI to generate both the pre-call agenda and the post-call summary directly from the filtered board view. What previously took 90 minutes of prep and follow-up per client now takes under five. The documents are consistent, professional, and ready to send — without leaving monday.com.

How to Get Started

If your board is not yet set up to support this, here is a quick column checklist to get there:

  • Item Name (your agenda topics)
  • Owner or People column (who presents or owns each item)
  • Status column (current state at time of meeting)
  • Due Date column (for prioritization and urgency)
  • Notes or Text column (context or pre-read material)
  • Decision column (post-meeting outcome)
  • Follow-Up Action column (next steps with owner)

Once those columns exist, your board is ready to generate documents automatically. You do not need a new system. You need a bridge between the structured data you already have and the formatted documents your clients and stakeholders expect.
