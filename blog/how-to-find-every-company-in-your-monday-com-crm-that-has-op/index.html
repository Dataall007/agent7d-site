How to Find Every Company in Your monday.com CRM That Has Open Deals and No Active Contact — and What That Silence Is Costing You

📅 October 7, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

There is a category of broken data in monday.com CRMs that nobody talks about enough: the orphaned account. A company record with active deals attached, pipeline value assigned, and absolutely no reachable human being connected to it. Nobody owns the relationship. Nobody is being emailed. The deal just sits there, aging quietly, while your revenue forecast stays optimistically inflated.

This is a monday.com CRM account hygiene problem, and it is more common than most revenue teams realize.

---

The Problem

An orphaned account looks healthy from a distance. It has a company name. It has deal value. It might even have a stage assigned — "Proposal Sent" or "Negotiation." But dig one level deeper and you find that the contact linked to that company left the organization six months ago, or was never properly entered, or got deleted when someone cleaned up duplicates without checking what was attached.

The company record exists. The deal exists. The relationship does not.

This matters because deals do not close themselves. Without a live contact who has authority and interest, every dollar attached to that company is theoretical. Worse, it is polluting your pipeline data — skewing your win rate calculations, inflating your forecast, and giving your team false confidence about where you actually stand.

---

The Solution

The fix is a structured account audit: a deliberate pass through your monday.com CRM that surfaces every company record where deals are active but no valid contact exists. Once you can see the list, you can make real decisions — chase, reassign, or close lost — instead of letting these records drift indefinitely.

monday.com gives you the tools to do this. The work is in knowing how to connect them.

---

Step by Step

1. Map your board relationships first. In a well-structured monday.com CRM, you have at least three boards: Companies, Contacts, and Deals. Your Contacts board should have a Connect Boards column linking each contact to a Company. Your Deals board should link to both. If this structure is not in place, that itself is an audit finding.

2. Create a filtered view on your Contacts board. Filter for contacts where the Status column equals something inactive — "Left company," "Unresponsive," "Invalid," or whatever your team uses. If you do not have a contact status column, add one now. That absence is the root cause of orphaned accounts.

3. Pull the linked companies from that filtered view. Using the Mirror column, surface the Company name for every inactive or missing contact. Export this as a CSV or use monday.com's dashboard widgets to aggregate it.

4. Cross-reference against your Deals board. Filter your Deals board for open deals — any stage that is not Closed Won or Closed Lost. Now compare the company list from step three against the companies attached to open deals. The overlap is your orphaned account list.

5. Work the list. For each orphaned account, assign an owner and give them one task: find a new contact within 14 days or close the deal as lost. No third option. Dead pipeline is worse than no pipeline.

---

Real Example

A B2B consulting firm with around 40 active accounts ran this process manually and found 17 company records where the linked contact had left the client organization — in most cases months earlier. Three of those companies had deals still marked as active, totaling $180,000 in combined value. No one had touched them. No follow-up had been sent. The deals were simply sitting there because no one had a system to notice they were broken.

Of the three, one deal was recoverable — the new stakeholder at that company was identified through LinkedIn, outreach was made, and the deal eventually closed. The other two were closed lost, which was uncomfortable but freed the team from forecasting revenue that was never going to arrive.

The real cost was not the $120,000 written off. It was the months of distorted forecasting that came before the audit.

---

How to Get Started

Before you build filters and mirror columns, you need to know how bad the problem actually is. For most teams, the orphaned account issue is just one symptom of broader monday.com CRM account hygiene problems — stuck deals, unowned records, missing fields that should be mandatory.

Auditing this manually takes hours. Knowing where to look takes experience most teams do not have in-house.
