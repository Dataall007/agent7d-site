The monday.com Renewal Letter You Should Be Sending — And How to Generate It Automatically

📅 August 26, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Most B2B account managers know the feeling. You open your calendar on a Monday morning and realize a client's contract renews in eleven days. You have no letter drafted, no approval started, and a blank document staring back at you. So you spend two hours pulling together pricing, contact names, and contract terms from three different places — and send something that reads like it was written in a hurry. Because it was.

This is monday.com contract renewal automation done wrong. Here's how to do it right.

---

The Problem

Renewal letters get written at the last minute because nothing forces them to happen earlier. The renewal date lives in a column on your monday.com board, but unless someone is actively watching it, it's just a number. No alert fires. No draft appears. The account manager only notices when the date is close enough to cause stress.

The cost isn't just the two hours of scrambling. It's the quality of the letter itself. A renewal sent nine days before expiry, written quickly from scratch, rarely reflects the client's actual history with you — their tier, their usage, their upsell potential. It's generic. And generic renewal letters get ignored or, worse, treated as an opening to shop around.

If you're managing 40, 60, or 80 recurring accounts, this problem compounds. You can't personally track every renewal date and produce tailored letters. Something will slip.

---

The Solution

The fix is to treat renewal letters as a document generation task, not a writing task. The data you need already exists inside monday.com. Your job is to connect that data to a document template and trigger the output automatically when a renewal date approaches.

That means two things working together: a date-based automation in monday.com that fires when a renewal column hits a threshold, and a document generation tool that pulls the relevant item data into a structured, professional letter the moment that trigger fires.

The letter doesn't get written from scratch. It gets generated from what you already know about the client.

---

Step by Step

1. Identify the five data points every renewal letter needs.

These are: client name, contract value, renewal date, primary contact, and one personalization detail (their current plan, product tier, or a relevant usage metric). All five should already be columns on your accounts board. If any are missing, add them now.

2. Set up a date-based automation trigger.

In monday.com, go to Automations and create a rule along the lines of: When renewal date arrives in 30 days, notify [owner] and change status to "Renewal Due." This is your signal that a letter needs to go out. Thirty days gives you time to review before sending.

3. Connect a document generation app to your board.

This is where DocuAI comes in. Once installed from the monday.com marketplace, you create a renewal letter template using your five data points as merge fields. When the automation fires, DocuAI pulls the current item's data and populates the template — client name, contract value, renewal date, and all.

4. Build a template that sounds human, not robotic.

Avoid filler phrases. Write the template as if you're addressing one specific client, use direct language, and leave one short paragraph as a editable block for any deal-specific context. The structure should be: acknowledge the relationship, confirm renewal terms, state the next step. That's it.

5. Review and send — don't rebuild.

Instead of editing a blank page, you open a fully populated draft and spend five minutes personalizing the editable block. Then send directly or push it to your email client. The difference between ninety minutes and five minutes is the template doing the work.

---

Real Example

A SaaS account management team runs eighty recurring client accounts on a monday.com board. Each item holds contract value, tier, renewal date, and account owner. Previously, renewal letters were written manually — each one took between one and two hours, and roughly 15% of renewals were missed or sent too late to influence the decision.

After setting up DocuAI with a renewal letter template tied to their existing board columns, the workflow changed: the automation fires thirty days out, a populated draft appears in the item, and the account manager spends a few minutes personalizing before sending. Renewal letters now go out consistently, on time, and with accurate client-specific details — without adding headcount.

---

How to Get Started

Open your monday.com accounts board and check whether your renewal date, contract value, and contact columns are consistently populated. If they are, you have everything you need. Create the date-based automation first — that alone will give you better visibility into upcoming renewals. Then install DocuAI, build your renewal letter template using your existing column data as merge fields, and run it against one upcoming renewal to see the output.

The goal is that the next time a renewal date approaches, the letter is already drafted before you even think to check.
