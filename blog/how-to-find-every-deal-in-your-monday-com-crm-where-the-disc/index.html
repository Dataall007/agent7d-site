How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Discount Was Applied But Never Approved

📅 October 20, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

If your reps can type a number into a discount field without any approval gate, some of them are doing it. Not because they're bad salespeople — because they're closing deals and discount is the easiest lever they have. The problem is your pipeline report will never flag it. Closed-won looks clean. Revenue looks fine. But your margin is bleeding, quietly, one unapproved 12% discount at a time.

This is a monday.com CRM discount audit problem, and it's more common than most sales leaders want to admit.

The Problem

Untracked discounts are simultaneously a pipeline health problem and a profitability problem, and that dual nature is exactly why they stay hidden for so long.

From a pipeline perspective, a deal with an unapproved discount is a deal where the data doesn't reflect the real commercial terms. Your weighted pipeline is based on deal values that may already be silently reduced. Forecasting accuracy breaks down. Finance is planning against numbers that are already wrong before the deal even closes.

From a profitability perspective, discounts without approval trails mean no one in leadership signed off on the margin concession. Finance was never looped in. No one checked whether that customer's deal size actually warranted the discount, whether it set a precedent with other accounts, or whether the rep had the authority to go that low.

The two problems compound each other. A discount applied at the deal level inflates your win rate while deflating your actual revenue per deal — and if you're not auditing for it, you won't see it until someone runs the numbers at quarter close and wonders why revenue is soft despite a strong close rate.

The Solution

The data pattern you're looking for is straightforward: any deal where a discount field is populated and an approval field is blank.

In monday.com, this typically means you have a Numbers or Text column for the discount percentage or amount, and either no approval column at all — which is its own problem — or an "Approved By," "Discount Status," or similar column that is simply empty on those records.

The fix has two phases. First, audit what already exists: find every deal in your board that matches this pattern. Second, redesign your board so the pattern can't repeat going forward.

Step by Step

1. Add or identify your discount and approval columns.

If your board has a discount column but no approval column, add one now — a People column for "Approved By" and a Status column for "Discount Status" (values: Not Applicable, Pending, Approved, Denied) will cover most use cases.

2. Filter your board to surface the gap.

Use monday.com's Filter function. Set Condition 1: Discount column is not empty (or greater than 0). Set Condition 2: Approved By column is empty — OR — Discount Status is not "Approved." Run the filter across all deal stages, not just open pipeline. Include closed-won.

3. Export the results.

Use the Export to Excel feature to get a clean list. Add the deal owner, deal value, close date, and discount amount to your export columns before running it. This becomes your audit list.

4. Reconstruct the approval history using activity logs.

For each flagged deal, open the item and check the Updates section and the column activity log (click the column header, select "Column History"). You're looking for whether anyone ever documented approval — in a comment, a mention, a status change — or whether the discount simply appeared with no record of discussion.

5. Redesign the board to prevent recurrence.

Use monday.com's Automations to create a trigger: when the Discount column is updated to any value above zero, automatically change the Discount Status to "Pending" and notify the sales manager. Use a column dependency or a workflow gate — deals should not be moveable to "Contract Sent" or "Closed Won" if Discount Status is still "Pending." This turns approval from a suggestion into a requirement.

Real Example

A managed services company did exactly this audit on their closed-won deals from the prior quarter. They filtered for deals with a populated discount field and no entry in their "Approved By" column. The result: 18% of closed deals included discounts with no approval on record. The average undocumented discount was 14%. Several of those deals involved significant contract values that finance had never been informed about. The sales team wasn't acting in bad faith — there was simply no system requiring them to do anything other than type a number into a field. The audit took one afternoon. The board redesign took another. The margin impact of fixing it was visible in the following quarter.
