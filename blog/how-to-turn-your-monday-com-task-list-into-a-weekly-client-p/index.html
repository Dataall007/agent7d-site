How to Turn Your monday.com Task List into a Weekly Client Progress Report Without Writing Anything

📅 October 10, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Every Friday afternoon, somewhere in the world, a project manager is staring at a monday.com board full of completed tasks, updated statuses, and comment threads — and then opening a blank Word document to type it all out again. Manually. For the fourth week in a row.

If that's you, this post is for you.

Target keyword: client progress report monday.com

---

The Problem

Clients don't live inside your monday.com boards. They want a clean, readable summary of what happened this week — not a screenshot of a Gantt chart or a share link to a board they'll never learn to navigate.

So someone on your team translates the board into English every week. They scroll through statuses, copy task names, check which items moved from "In Progress" to "Done," summarize blockers in plain language, and format it all into something that looks professional enough to send.

For a single client, that's 30–45 minutes. For six clients, that's half a Friday.

Aziz, a project manager at a commercial fit-out company in Dubai, managed six concurrent office renovation projects. Every Friday, he opened six separate report templates — each client had slightly different preferences for layout and detail — and populated them one by one from his monday.com boards. It was the most draining, low-value part of his week. The data already existed. He was just a very expensive copy-paste machine.

---

The Solution

The solution isn't a better template. It's eliminating the manual transfer entirely.

Your monday.com board already holds everything a client progress report needs: task names, statuses, assigned owners, dates, and update logs. The only missing piece is a tool that reads that data and turns it into a formatted, client-ready document automatically.

DocuAI is a monday.com app that does exactly that. You configure a report template once — your logo, your sections, your language — and then generate a polished PDF from any board's live data in seconds. No copying. No formatting. No Friday afternoon lost to document assembly.

---

Step by Step

Here's how to set up a reporting workflow that actually runs itself.

1. Structure your board for reporting

Make sure each item has a clear status column (Done / In Progress / Blocked / Not Started), an owner column, a week column or date, and a notes/update field. If your board already has these, you're 90% there.

2. Filter before you generate

Use monday.com's native filter to isolate this week's items — filter by date range, status, or any label that marks something as current. You're not reporting on the entire project history, just the past seven days.

3. Map your template in DocuAI

In DocuAI, build your progress report template using dynamic fields that pull directly from your board columns. Task names, statuses, owner names, completion percentages — all pulled automatically. Set it once.

4. Handle the "nothing moved" sections honestly

Good progress reports don't skip items that didn't progress — they acknowledge them. Use a conditional section in DocuAI that outputs a brief "no updates this period" note when a task group hasn't changed. Clients notice when you skip items. This builds trust.

5. Generate and send

Select the filtered view, hit generate, and DocuAI produces a formatted PDF in seconds. Send it directly from monday.com or download and attach to your client email.

Real Example

Back to Aziz. After setting up DocuAI with a single master progress report template, he mapped each client's board to the same template structure. On Fridays, his new workflow: apply the week filter on each board, generate the report, send. Six reports in under fifteen minutes total.

The 45-minute-per-client process became a two-minute one.
