How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Pipeline Stage and the Last Activity Are More Than 30 Days Apart — and What It Means for Your Revenue

📅 November 3, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Your monday.com pipeline looks healthy. Deals are spread across stages. The board is full. Revenue feels predictable.

It isn't.

There's a specific type of pipeline rot that's almost invisible until it's too late — and it has nothing to do with deals moving backwards. It's deals that stopped moving entirely, while their stage label kept telling you they were fine.

The Problem

Stage columns in monday.com reflect the last thing a rep did to a deal. They don't reflect what the deal is doing right now.

When a rep moves a deal to "Proposal Sent," that status stays there indefinitely — whether the prospect replied yesterday or went silent six weeks ago. The board shows progress. Your pipeline review shows coverage. But underneath, nothing is happening.

This is stage progression without activity, and it's the most dangerous form of pipeline rot because it doesn't trigger any alerts. The deal doesn't look stuck. It looks in-progress. It gets counted in your weighted forecast. It occupies rep attention that should go elsewhere. And when it finally dies — usually quietly, with no formal close-lost — you lose both the deal and the visibility into why.

The 30-day threshold matters because it's long enough to be intentional. A two-week gap might be a holiday, a delayed procurement cycle, or a rep finishing a run of outbound. Thirty days is a pattern. It means either the rep stopped working the deal, the prospect went dark, or both — and neither is a situation where your current stage label is telling you the truth.

The Solution

You need to cross-reference two things your monday.com board almost certainly already tracks: the Pipeline Stage column and the Last Activity Date column (or equivalent — sometimes labeled "Last Updated," "Last Contact," or "Last Log").

The goal is to surface every deal where the stage implies forward momentum but the activity date says otherwise. Once you have that list, you stop managing a pipeline and start managing reality.

Step by Step

1. Confirm your activity date field exists and is being used.

Go to your CRM board and check whether reps are logging calls, emails, or meetings as updates or through a dedicated date column. If that column is empty for most deals, you have a data hygiene problem before you have a pipeline problem — fix logging discipline first.

2. Add a formula column to calculate the gap.

In monday.com, add a Formula column and calculate the difference between today and the Last Activity Date. Something like TODAY() - {Last Activity Date} gives you days since last contact as a live number.

3. Filter for deals where gap > 30 and stage is not "Closed Won" or "Closed Lost."

Use monday.com's filter functionality to isolate exactly the deals in an active stage with a 30+ day activity gap. Save this as a named view: call it "Frozen Pipeline" or "At-Risk Deals."

4. Group by stage.

This tells you where in the funnel your frozen deals are clustering. "Proposal Sent" and "Negotiation" are the most common — and the most expensive — because those are high-value, late-stage deals where stalling is fatal.

5. Add deal value to the view and sum the column.

This is the moment most sales leaders find uncomfortable. That total is the revenue your pipeline is misrepresenting right now.

Real Example

A SaaS sales team ran this exact exercise on their monday.com board. They filtered for deals in "Proposal Sent" with no logged activity in 45 days or more. They found 14 deals. Combined value: $180,000.

None of these deals had appeared in their weekly at-risk review, because the stage label — "Proposal Sent" — read as active. They looked like deals waiting on a prospect decision. In reality, eight of them had reps who hadn't followed up at all, four had prospects who'd stopped responding, and two were genuine procurement holds with no documented next step.

Three of the 14 converted after direct outreach with a revised offer. Two were formally closed lost, which actually improved forecast accuracy. The other nine were reassigned or escalated. The team recovered roughly $60k in pipeline that would otherwise have silently decayed — and they fixed a forecasting blind spot that had been distorting their numbers for months.

How to Get Started

The manual version of this audit is doable, but it depends entirely on whether your activity data is clean, your formula columns are set up correctly, and your team is disciplined enough to maintain both. Most monday.com boards aren't fully there yet — which is why the first pass almost always surfaces more problems than expected.

Start by building the "Frozen Pipeline" view described above. Run it today. Look at the total value sitting in that grey zone. Then decide whether what you're seeing is a rep coaching issue, a follow-up process problem, a data quality issue, or a forecasting methodology problem — because the stage-activity gap will tell you which one.

The answer is almost always "more than one."
