How to Auto-Generate a Client Offboarding Document from Your monday.com Board When a Project Ends

📅 November 6, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

The Problem

The last thing a client experiences with your team shapes how they talk about you afterward. And for most agencies and service teams, that final moment is… nothing. No formal summary, no handover package, no written record of what was actually delivered. The project just quietly closes, someone sends a "great working with you" email, and that's it.

This is a real missed opportunity — and a risk.

Without a proper monday.com client offboarding document, clients leave with unanswered questions: Which version of the file was final? Who holds the login credentials? Was that last revision included in scope? Months later, those questions turn into awkward emails or, worse, disputes.

The irony is that most teams invest heavily in onboarding. There are welcome emails, kickoff decks, intake forms, and structured workflows. Offboarding gets none of that attention, even though it directly affects referrals, reviews, and repeat business.

---

The Solution

By the time a project closes, your monday.com board already holds most of what a proper offboarding document needs. Completed tasks, delivered files, assigned contacts, status histories, sign-off checkboxes — it's all sitting in columns and groups, just not assembled into anything a client can actually read or sign.

The solution is to stop treating offboarding as a manual task and start treating it as a triggered output. When a board status changes to "Complete" or "Closed," generate the offboarding document automatically, pulling directly from the data already in the board. No copy-pasting. No forgotten items. No inconsistent formatting.

This is exactly what DocuAI does. It connects to your monday.com board, reads the relevant columns, and assembles a structured document the moment you need it.

---

Step by Step

1. Decide what your offboarding document should contain.

A strong client offboarding document typically includes: a deliverables summary (what was completed and when), a list of any open or deferred items, an asset and login transfer checklist, a formal sign-off section, and a brief section on next steps or future engagement options. Each of these maps directly to data you're already tracking.

2. Identify the right columns and groups in your board.

Look at your board through the lens of project end state. Completed task groups give you the deliverables summary. File attachment columns give you the asset list. Status and date columns document the timeline. People columns identify assigned contacts for handover. If you're already running a disciplined board, 80% of the content is already there.

3. Map columns to document sections.

In DocuAI, you create a document template and define which monday.com columns feed each section. The deliverables summary pulls from your "Done" group. The handover checklist pulls from a dedicated checklist column. The client contact section pulls from the people field. This mapping only needs to be set up once per board template.

4. Set a trigger based on status.

Configure DocuAI to generate the document automatically when your project status column hits "Complete" or "Closed." The document is created instantly, without anyone having to remember to run it. You can also set it to notify the project owner when it's ready for review before sending.

5. Send it to the client.

The generated document is clean, branded, and ready to share — either as a PDF or via a shareable link. The client receives a professional record of the engagement, not a rushed email with scattered attachments.
