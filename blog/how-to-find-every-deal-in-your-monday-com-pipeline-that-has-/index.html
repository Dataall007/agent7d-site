How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com Pipeline That Has Been Active for More Than 90 Days Without a Single Stage Change

📅 September 26, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

A deal that hasn't moved in 90 days isn't a live opportunity. It's a placeholder with a dollar value attached to it, and it's quietly inflating your pipeline while giving you a false sense of what's actually closable this quarter. If your forecast is built on numbers that include these deals, your forecast is wrong.

Here's how to find every one of them in monday.com, why they stalled, and what to do about each one.

---

The Problem

Stuck deals are the most dangerous kind of pipeline pollution — not because they're obviously broken, but because they look fine. They have a stage. They have a value. They have an owner. On a dashboard, they register as healthy. But nothing has happened in three months.

Ninety days is the threshold where a stalled deal stops being "slow" and starts being "probably dead." At that point, the contact has almost certainly moved on mentally, the internal champion may have changed, and the competitive landscape has shifted. Treating these records as real pipeline doesn't just distort your forecast — it affects where reps focus their time and what leadership believes is coming in.

The longer these deals stay on the board unchallenged, the harder it becomes to make accurate revenue decisions. That's the real cost.

---

The Solution

monday.com gives you enough filtering and formula capability to surface every stuck deal in your pipeline in under ten minutes. The goal is to build a filtered view that isolates any deal where the stage column has not changed in more than 90 days, flag the underlying cause, and then make a clean decision: revive, reassign, or close as lost.

Once you've run this process once, it becomes a recurring habit — not a crisis cleanup.

---

Step by Step

1. Add a "Last Stage Change" date column to your board.

If you don't already track this, add a Date column called "Stage Last Updated." Use a monday.com automation to populate it: When Status changes, set Date to today. Run this going forward and manually audit older records.

2. Build a filtered view called "Stalled 90+ Days."

Filter your board to show only deals where:

  • Stage Last Updated is more than 90 days ago
  • Deal Stage is not Closed Won or Closed Lost

Save this as a dedicated view so it's always one click away.

3. Add a "Next Action" column if you don't have one.

Sort the filtered view by this column. Deals with no next action logged are your highest-priority problem records.

4. Tag the cause before you do anything else.

For each stalled deal, add a label or note identifying why it stalled. The four most common causes: ghosted contact, internal blocker on the buyer's side, wrong rep assigned (including reps who've left), or the deal is already dead but was never officially closed. Getting this on the record first keeps your data clean regardless of what decision you make next.

5. Triage into one of three buckets.

  • Revive: There's still a real contact, a real budget signal, and a plausible path to close. Assign a specific next action with a due date.
  • Reassign: The deal has merit but is with the wrong rep — including any assigned to someone who's no longer on the team.
  • Close Lost: No contact response in 90+ days, no budget confirmed, no next step possible. Mark it closed lost with a reason. This is not failure — it's accuracy.

6. Remove duplicates before closing.

Cross-check against your Closed Won records. Duplicate deals inflate pipeline twice: once when they're open, once when they're never cleaned up.

---

Real Example

A SaaS sales team ran this exact audit and found 22 deals totaling $340,000 sitting at "Proposal Sent" — untouched for over three months. Not one had a next action logged. Four were assigned to a rep who had left the company two months earlier. Two were duplicates of deals already marked Closed Won.

Their reported pipeline showed $340K in late-stage opportunity. Their actual pipeline showed something much smaller. The difference wasn't a bad quarter — it was bad data that had never been challenged.

After triaging, they revived six deals, reassigned three, and closed thirteen as lost. Their pipeline shrank on paper. Their forecast accuracy improved significantly.

---

How to Get Started

To prevent 90-day stalls from accumulating in the first place, build two automations into your monday.com board: one that sends a rep notification when a deal hasn't changed stage in 30 days, and one that flags the record in a stalled view at 60 days. Catching deals at 30 days costs you a five-minute conversation. Catching them at 90 days costs you a full audit.

The filter view you built above? Schedule a team review of it once a month. Put it on the calendar. Make it the first agenda item in your pipeline review meeting. Stale deals accumulate in silence — the only way to stop them is to look deliberately.
