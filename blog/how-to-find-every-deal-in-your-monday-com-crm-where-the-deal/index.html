How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Deal Was Won But No Revenue Was Ever Recorded — and Why It's Making Your Closed Revenue Look Smaller Than It Is

📅 November 7, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

The Problem

There is a quiet data problem sitting inside most monday.com CRMs, and it has nothing to do with lost deals or bad forecasts. It is about won deals — specifically, deals that were moved to Closed Won without anyone filling in the final contract value.

This happens constantly. A rep closes a deal on a Friday afternoon, moves the item to the Closed Won stage, sends the contract, and moves on. The revenue field stays empty. Nobody flags it because the status looks correct. The deal is won — it just has no number attached to it.

Multiply that across a quarter with a team of five reps and you can easily end up with seven or eight won deals sitting at zero revenue. When your sales manager pulls the board report, the closed revenue total looks 20% lighter than reality — not because performance was weaker, but because the data was never entered.

The core reasons this happens are predictable. Revenue or deal value fields are rarely mandatory in monday.com by default. Reps are measured on stage movement, not field completeness. And when a deal closes after a negotiation with a revised contract value, the original estimate in the board never gets updated to the final number. Everyone knows the deal closed. Nobody goes back to fix the record.

The result is a reporting problem disguised as a revenue problem.

The Solution

The fix is straightforward once you can see which records are affected. The goal is to filter your monday.com board so that every item in a Closed Won status where the revenue column is empty or contains a zero value becomes visible in one place.

Before you do that, it is worth distinguishing between two different situations. Some deals genuinely have no monetary value — pilot programs, internal accounts, goodwill engagements. Those should have a note or a zero logged deliberately, so the absence of a number is intentional and documented. The dangerous records are the ones where a real revenue figure exists on a signed contract somewhere but never made it into the board.

Your job is to surface all zero or empty revenue items in Closed Won, then triage them into two buckets: intentionally zero and accidentally empty. The first bucket is fine. The second one is what you need to fix before your next report runs.

Step by Step

Here is how to run this audit manually in monday.com today.

1. Open your CRM board and add a filter. Set the first filter condition to your status column equals Closed Won. Set the second filter condition to your revenue or deal value column is empty. Run the filter. Every record that appears is a won deal with no revenue logged.

2. Add a second view for zero values. Repeat the same filter but change the revenue condition to "equals 0" rather than empty. Some boards use a default zero instead of leaving fields blank, so you need both passes to catch everything.

3. Export or screenshot the results. You want a stable list you can work from, not a live filter that changes as you update records. Export to Excel or take a column screenshot.

4. Cross-reference against your contracts or invoices. For each flagged item, check the actual closed value. If a real number exists, update the board record immediately and note when the correction was made.

5. Add a mandatory revenue field for future items. Go to your board settings and make the deal value column required before an item can move to Closed Won. monday.com does not enforce this natively at the column level, but you can use automations or column permissions to create a friction point that prompts reps to fill it in before stage movement.

6. Set a recurring audit reminder. Add a weekly recurring task to check for Closed Won items with empty or zero revenue. Catching this weekly takes two minutes. Catching it at quarter-end after the report is already distributed is a different conversation entirely.

Real Example

A sales manager at a twelve-person SaaS company pulls her Q2 revenue report from monday.com. The board shows $340,000 in closed revenue. Her gut says the number should be closer to $410,000 — she knows what the team closed, she was on most of the calls.

She runs a filter for Closed Won items with an empty deal value column. Seven records appear. She cross-references against the signed contracts in her CRM files. All seven deals have real contract values. The combined total is $68,000 that never made it into the board. Her closed revenue was not $340,000 — it was $408,000. The board was not wrong about the wins. It was just missing the numbers.

The issue was not negligence. Two deals had their values revised during negotiation and nobody updated the board after the final contract was signed. Four more were closed by a rep who routinely moved stage first and planned to fill in the value later. One had a data entry error that left the field blank instead of populated.

The report went to leadership showing a shortfall that did not exist.
