How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Deal Was Marked Closed Won But the Project Board Was Never Created — and What That Gap Is Costing You

📅 November 17, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Closing a deal feels like the finish line. It isn't. For services businesses, closing a deal is the starting gun for delivery — and if that gun never fires, the race never starts. What you get instead is a CRM item sitting in your Closed Won column while nothing actually happens.

This is one of the quietest ways revenue leaks in monday.com pipelines. No error message. No alert. Just a won deal that never became a project, and a client who eventually wonders why no one has called.

---

The Problem

In monday.com, your CRM pipeline and your project workspace are separate environments. That separation is useful — until it isn't.

The gap between a deal being marked Closed Won and a project board being created is not always a problem. There's legitimate lag: contracts need signing, kickoff calls need scheduling, resources need assigning. Normal handoff lag is measured in days.

The problem is when the lag is measured in weeks — or the handoff never happens at all. A deal marked Closed Won with no linked project board after 14 days is not in transition. It's stuck.

This matters for three concrete reasons. First, revenue recognition: if your accounting team bills on project milestones, uninitiated projects mean unbilled work. Second, delivery risk: scope lives in the CRM item — discovery notes, attachments, agreed deliverables — and if delivery team never receives that context, they're guessing. Third, client experience: the client signed. They're waiting. The longer the silence, the worse the relationship starts.

The real problem underneath this isn't laziness. It's structural. monday.com doesn't natively enforce a link between a CRM item and a project board. Nothing breaks when that link doesn't exist. So it routinely doesn't get created.

---

The Solution

You need a cross-reference audit: a systematic comparison of every Closed Won deal in your CRM board against every active project board in your workspace.

The goal is to produce a list of CRM items in Closed Won status that have no corresponding project board — no linked item, no connected board, no reference in a connected column. That list is your gap report.

Once you have it, you can prioritize by close date, assign accountability, and calculate the total contract value sitting in that gap. That number, in most pipelines we've audited, surprises people.

---

Step by Step

1. Export your Closed Won deals. Filter your CRM board by status: Closed Won. Export to Excel or use monday's column filters to isolate these items. Note the deal name, owner, close date, and contract value for each.

2. Pull your active project boards. In your monday.com workspace, list every board in your project delivery workspace. If you use a consistent naming convention (client name + project name, for example), this is faster. If you don't, now you know why you should.

3. Cross-reference manually or by connected column. If your CRM items have a "linked project" column, filter for items where that column is empty and status is Closed Won. If you don't have that column, you're doing this by hand: match deal names against board names. Flag every deal with no match.

4. Flag by age. Sort unlinked deals by close date. Any deal closed more than 14 days ago with no project board is a priority flag. Any deal closed more than 30 days ago is an active revenue risk.

5. Calculate uninitiated contract value. Sum the contract values of every unlinked Closed Won deal. That figure is revenue your business has technically earned but not yet started delivering — and therefore has not yet fully secured.

---

Real Example

A digital services agency ran this audit across their Q1 pipeline. They had 14 deals marked Closed Won. When they cross-referenced against their project workspace, 6 had no corresponding project board.

Of those 6: two had never been resourced at all — no team member had been assigned, no kickoff scheduled, no client contacted post-signature. One had been billed based on a preliminary estimate in the CRM item rather than a finalized scope, resulting in an invoice that was $4,200 short of the actual agreed value. The discrepancy only surfaced when the client queried a change order three months into delivery.

The other three were genuine lag cases — projects in pre-kickoff — but had no documented timeline, so there was no way to tell from the CRM data alone whether they were progressing or stalled.

Total uninitiated contract value across the 6 unlinked deals: $112,000. Not lost. Not cancelled. Just sitting in a column, unexecuted.

---

How to Get Started

The manual version of this audit takes a few hours for a typical pipeline. The bigger investment is deciding what your post-close workflow should actually look like in monday.com — which columns are required before a deal can move to Closed Won, whether you automate project board creation on status change, and who owns the handoff.

A clean post-close workflow in monday.com typically includes: a required "linked project board" column that must be populated before Closed Won status is available, an automation that pings the delivery lead when a deal moves to Closed Won, and a weekly view filtered to show Closed Won deals with empty project links — so the gap never grows past seven days undetected.

That last piece — the ongoing visibility — is what most teams skip. They fix the backlog once and don't build the monitoring that prevents the next one.
