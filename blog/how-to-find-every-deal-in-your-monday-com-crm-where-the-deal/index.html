How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Deal Was Marked Won But No Project or Delivery Board Was Ever Created — and What That Gap Is Costing You

📅 November 18, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Closing a deal should be the beginning of something, not the end of your visibility into it. But in monday.com CRMs that weren't set up with a clear handoff path, "Closed Won" is often exactly where the trail goes cold — and where real revenue starts to disappear quietly.

The Problem

There is a gap that sits between your deals board and your delivery boards, and most monday.com teams don't know it exists until a client calls to ask why nothing has started.

Here's how it happens: a rep marks a deal Closed Won, updates the status column, and moves on to the next prospect. The deal looks clean in the pipeline. It's won. It's in the books. But no one created a project board. No delivery item was ever linked. No task was assigned. The work that the client paid for — or is about to pay for — never actually got scoped.

This is one of the most expensive silent leaks in a monday.com CRM. It doesn't show up as a lost deal. It doesn't trigger an alert. It sits in your Closed Won group looking like a success while the client on the other end waits, loses confidence, and eventually churns — sometimes before a single deliverable is discussed.

The disconnect has three typical causes. First, the handoff is manual and nobody did it. Second, the automation that was supposed to create a linked project item was never built or broke at some point. Third, the rep who closed the deal had no ownership of the delivery side and assumed someone else would pick it up.

The Solution

You need to cross-reference your deals board against your delivery or project boards and find every Closed Won deal that has no linked items, no connected board, and no subitems representing scoped work.

Monday.com gives you the data to do this — it just doesn't surface the gap automatically. The fix involves a manual audit first, followed by structural changes so the gap can't reopen.

Step by Step

1. Filter your deals board to Closed Won. Sort by close date and pull every deal marked won in your target period — last quarter is a good starting point.

2. Check the linked item column. If you use board linking between your CRM and your project or delivery boards, look for any Closed Won deals where the linked items column is empty. No link means no delivery board was connected.

3. Cross-reference by deal name or client. Go to your project or delivery boards and search for the client or deal name. If you can't find a matching item, that deal never made it into delivery.

4. Check subitems. Some teams scope work as subitems directly on the deal rather than creating a separate board item. If subitems are also empty on a Closed Won deal, nothing was scoped there either.

5. Add a "Delivery Status" column to your deals board. Create a simple status column — Not Started, In Progress, Delivered — and populate it manually during your audit. This becomes your ongoing visibility layer.

6. Calculate the revenue in the gap. Sum the deal values for every Closed Won item with no linked delivery record. That number represents revenue you've recognized or are expecting that has no operational plan behind it.

Real Example

A services agency ran a Q1 review of their monday.com CRM and found eleven deals marked Closed Won. When they cross-referenced against their project board, four of those deals had no linked project items at all — no delivery board, no scoped tasks, no assigned owner on the work side.

Of those four, two clients had already churned. Both had signed contracts, never received a proper kickoff, and left before anyone on the internal team realized the delivery process had never started. The agency had closed the deals correctly. They had simply never built the bridge to the work.

The combined value of those four deals was just under $60,000. The two churned clients represented $28,000 that was lost entirely, not because the work was bad, but because the work never began.

How to Get Started

Start with the manual audit described above — it takes less than an hour for most pipelines and will show you immediately whether this gap exists in your CRM. Once you've found the affected deals, prioritize building a monday.com automation that triggers a project item creation the moment a deal status changes to Closed Won. Add an ownership rule so the delivery lead is assigned automatically. Remove the manual handoff entirely.

Then look at the rest of your pipeline. This leak rarely travels alone.
