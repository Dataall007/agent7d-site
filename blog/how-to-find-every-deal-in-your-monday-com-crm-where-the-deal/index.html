How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Deal Was Won But No Revenue Was Ever Recorded — and What That Gap Is Doing to Your Actual Numbers

📅 November 14, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Winning a deal feels like the finish line. The status flips to Won, the rep moves on, and everyone assumes the number is in the system. It usually isn't.

This is one of the most common and least-discussed data problems in monday.com CRMs: closed-won items sitting in your pipeline with no revenue value attached. Your deal count looks right. Your win rate looks fine. But your actual recorded revenue is quietly short — and nobody notices until the quarterly review lands wrong or finance asks where the numbers went.

Here's how to find every one of those deals and fix them.

---

The Problem

Why won deals end up with no revenue recorded in monday.com

The gap happens for three reasons, and they're all predictable.

First, reps close in conversation, not in the CRM. The verbal yes comes in, the rep updates the status to Won, and that's the last thing they touch. The deal value column — whatever you've named it — never gets filled. The momentum is gone, the next deal is open, and the number just never makes it in.

Second, your board doesn't enforce it. monday.com doesn't require a revenue value before a status change. There's no native validation that blocks a deal from moving to Won if the amount column is empty or zero. Unless you've built that gate yourself, it doesn't exist.

Third, deals sometimes get duplicated or migrated. A deal that started as a lead on one board gets re-created on a deals board mid-process, and the revenue value that existed on the original item never transfers over. The Won item on the pipeline board is a shell.

None of this is a rep discipline problem. It's a system design problem. And the fix lives in the system.

---

The Solution

How to filter your monday.com CRM to surface every closed-won item with a missing or zero revenue value

monday.com's filter logic handles this directly — you just need to stack two conditions.

Go to your deals board. Open the filter panel. Set the first condition to your status column: is Won (or whatever your closed-won label is). Set the second condition to your revenue or deal value column: is empty OR is equal to 0.

Apply both filters together. What you're left with is your problem list — every item your team considered closed but never actually recorded a number for.

If you have deals spread across multiple boards or use Groups to separate pipeline stages, run the filter on each board where Won deals can land. Don't assume they're all in one place.

---

Step by Step

Running a one-time cleanup and setting up a forward-looking automation

  • Run the filter described above and export or screenshot the full list before you touch anything.
  • Assign ownership of the cleanup. Each rep should update their own items — they're more likely to know the actual value than anyone else.
  • For deals where the rep is gone or the value genuinely can't be verified, mark the revenue field with a placeholder (many teams use $1 as a flag) and add a note column entry that flags it as unverified.
  • Once the board is clean, go to Automations and create this rule: When status changes to Won, and deal value is empty, notify [owner] and assigned manager. monday.com's automation center supports conditional triggers — you can set "if column is empty" as the condition.
  • Add a secondary automation: When status changes to Won, send a reminder to the item owner after 24 hours if deal value is still empty. This creates a soft deadline without blocking the rep's workflow.

    • That combination — one-time cleanup plus a forward-looking automation — closes the gap in both directions.

    Real Example

    What this looks like when it costs you

    A marketing agency closed 14 deals in Q2. Their pipeline summary at the board level showed $340,000 in won revenue. When their ops lead ran the filter above before the finance handoff, she found 5 deals marked Won with zero or blank revenue values.

    Those 5 deals represented roughly $87,000 in actual closed business — deals the reps remembered, with contracts signed, but with nothing in the board. The number that went to finance was $253,000. The forecast for Q3 was built on that lower figure. Hiring decisions were made on it.

    The fix took two hours of cleanup. The cost of not fixing it was a quarter of planning built on incomplete data.

    This is not unusual. Five out of fourteen is high, but two or three missing values on a 20-deal quarter is completely normal for teams that haven't built the enforcement layer.

    ---

    How to Get Started

    If this post made you want to check your own board before reading another word — good instinct. The filter takes 90 seconds to set up. Run it now, before you read anything else.

    What you find will tell you a lot about your pipeline's data hygiene. If the list is empty, your team is unusually disciplined. If it's not, you now know where your numbers are leaking and exactly what to do about it.

    The minimum a closed-won deal should have before it leaves your pipeline: a recorded revenue value, a close date, a named owner, and a linked contact or company. If any of those are missing, the deal isn't done — it's just status-labeled.
