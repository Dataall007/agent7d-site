How to Find Every Deal in Your monday.com CRM Where the Deal Was Won But No Revenue Was Ever Recorded — and Why Your Closed Numbers Are Lying to You

📅 November 9, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Your monday.com dashboard says you closed 40 deals last quarter. Leadership is happy. Commission is calculated. The board gets a clean number.

But what if a third of those wins have no revenue attached to them at all?

This is one of the most common and least-discussed data problems in monday.com pipelines. Closed-won deals with blank deal values — ghost wins — are quietly inflating your conversion metrics while making your actual revenue figures meaningless. Here is how to find them, why they exist, and how to stop them from happening again.

---

The Problem

Moving a deal to "Won" in monday.com is a stage change. It is a dropdown selection, a column update, a click. It does not automatically confirm, verify, or record the revenue that was actually sold.

This distinction matters enormously. Most sales teams treat a closed-won status as proof that revenue was captured. It is not. It is proof that someone changed a status column. The deal value, expected revenue, or contract amount field — whatever your team uses — is entirely separate. And it is left blank far more often than sales leaders realise.

The result: your closed-won total is a count of status changes, not a sum of verified revenue. When those numbers diverge, every downstream metric built on them — win rate, average deal size, revenue forecast, commission calculations — becomes unreliable.

---

The Solution

You need to separate two distinct data points that your monday.com CRM currently conflates: status and value. A deal is only meaningfully closed when both are confirmed. Any item marked Won where the value column is blank or zero is not a closed deal — it is an incomplete record that needs immediate attention.

The fix has two phases. First, audit what already exists. Find every ghost win in your current pipeline and either confirm the revenue or reclassify the record. Second, build a process that prevents new ghost wins from ever reaching closed-won status without a confirmed value.

---

Step by Step

1. Identify your revenue column. In your monday.com CRM board, locate the column that holds deal value — commonly labelled Deal Value, Revenue, Contract Amount, or similar. If you have more than one, decide which is the authoritative figure.

2. Filter for Won deals. Use monday.com's filter functionality to show only items where your status column equals "Won" (or your equivalent closed-won label).

3. Add a secondary filter for blank or zero values. Filter that same view for items where your revenue column is blank or contains zero. This is your ghost wins list.

4. Export and triage. Export this filtered view to Excel or use monday.com's native export. For each record, determine: does real revenue exist for this deal and was simply never logged, or was this deal won at zero value (a legitimate free or pilot close)?

5. Update records with confirmed values. Go back to each item and enter the correct revenue. Add a note or activity log entry recording when and why the correction was made.

6. Create an automation to prevent recurrence. In monday.com's Automation Center, build a rule that sends a notification — or blocks the status change entirely — when someone attempts to move a deal to Won while the revenue column is empty. This creates a mandatory checkpoint at the moment of close.

---

Real Example

A SaaS sales team runs this audit at the end of Q3. They have 40 deals marked Won. When they filter for items where the deal value field is blank or zero, they find 14 records — 35% of their closed pipeline — with no revenue logged at all.

After investigation, eight of those deals have confirmed contract values that were simply never entered. The other six are free trials that converted and were marked Won prematurely before pricing was agreed.

The corrected revenue total is 22% lower than the number that had been reported to the board. Their actual win rate, once ghost wins are reclassified, drops from 40% to 31%. Commission calculations need to be revisited. The forecast for Q4, which was extrapolated from Q3 performance, is now based on accurate data for the first time in months.

Fourteen missing entries. Significant downstream damage.
