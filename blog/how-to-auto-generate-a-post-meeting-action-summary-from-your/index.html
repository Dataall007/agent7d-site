How to Auto-Generate a Post-Meeting Action Summary from Your monday.com Items After Every Client Call

📅 October 23, 2026 ✍️ agent7d Team ⏱ 5 min read

Every client call produces a list of actions that someone has to type up and send — usually hours later, usually incomplete. Here's how to pull those actions directly from your monday.com board and turn them into a formatted, client-ready summary without writing a word.

---

The Problem

Picture this: it's 4:30 PM. You just wrapped a 90-minute client call. You took notes in a meeting, your colleague updated a few items in monday.com mid-call, and now you're sitting down to write the follow-up summary before the day ends. Forty minutes later, you send a polished email recapping decisions, owners, and deadlines — only for the client to reply the next morning asking who owns the landing page copy and when the revised brief is due.

This is the reality for most project managers at agencies and consulting firms. The monday.com post-meeting action summary is arguably the most important deliverable after a client call, yet it's entirely manual, consistently delayed, and almost always missing something.

The irony? Every piece of information needed to build that summary already exists in monday.com. You're just not using it.

---

The Solution

Your monday.com board already knows everything that matters after a client call: which tasks were flagged, who owns them, what the status is, and when things are due. The problem isn't a lack of data — it's the disconnection between that data and a formatted, human-readable document.

The fix is to map your existing monday.com columns to a standard action summary format and trigger document generation automatically whenever a status changes or a date column is hit. No copy-pasting. No rewriting. No waiting until tomorrow.

DocuAI for monday.com connects directly to your board data and generates structured documents — including post-meeting action summaries — based on the items, columns, and values you define. The document is built the moment the trigger fires.

---

Step by Step

1. Define your action summary columns

Make sure your board has the columns that belong in every post-meeting summary: Task Name, Owner (People column), Due Date, Status, Priority, and a Notes or Context text column. If your team already updates these mid-call, you're set.

2. Create a group for meeting actions

During or immediately after each call, move flagged items into a dedicated group — "Meeting Actions — [Client Name]" works well. This scopes your document to only the items that were discussed.

3. Map columns to your document template in DocuAI

In DocuAI, build a template with sections that mirror a readable summary: a header with the meeting date and client name, a table of action items with owner and due date, a status overview, and a next steps section. Each field pulls directly from the corresponding monday.com column.

4. Set your automation trigger

Use a monday.com automation — or DocuAI's built-in trigger settings — to fire document generation when a Status column changes to "Meeting Reviewed" or when a Date column matches today. The summary generates automatically without anyone clicking a button.

5. Send directly from monday.com

DocuAI produces the document as a PDF or shareable link. Wire this into a monday.com notification or integration to email it to your client contact without leaving the platform.

How to Get Started

Start with one board and one meeting type. Map your existing columns — don't create new ones just for this. Build a clean template in DocuAI that mirrors the summary you'd normally write by hand: header, action table, owners, dates, next steps. Set a single status-change trigger. Run it on your next client call and compare the output to what you would have written manually.

The goal isn't to replace judgment — it's to eliminate the part of your job that's just transcribing information that already exists.
